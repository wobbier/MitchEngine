# Networking

Multiplayer follows the Overwatch model: an authoritative server, client prediction, and C# first. It is built in steps, NET-0 to NET-11 on the roadmap. What exists so far is the foundation:

- **NET-0:** a deterministic character step and its replayable state (see [Physics.md](Physics.md)). Scripts can act on a scoped world (see [Scripting-DotNet.md](Scripting-DotNet.md)).
- **NET-1:** the wire layer: the bit stream, quantization, transports, and a connection with acks, round trip, loss and a reliable ordered stream. It's pure C++ with no engine dependencies beyond the math types.
- **NET-2:** sessions:
  - the handshake, with a version check;
  - command frames, sent redundantly into the server's per-client buffers;
  - the client clock, with dilation;
  - the engine-owned `NetCore` in the frame;
  - a two-world test harness.

- **NET-3:** replication of `NetIdentity` entities:
  - static and dynamic NetIds;
  - reliable spawns and despawns;
  - delta snapshots built with `NetSchema` from reflection;
  - client interpolation;
  - kinematic remotes.

- **NET-4:** client prediction of the owner's character, with rollback, resim and error smoothing.
- **NET-8:** lag compensation: `NetHitbox`, a rewound `LagCompensatedRaycast`, and its overlays.
- **NET-13:** relevancy, a priority accumulator, a per-client bandwidth budget, and snapshot encoding on jobs.
- **NET-5:** real networking:
  - `UdpTransport`, plain UDP over IPv4 (no ENet: `NetConnection` already does the reliability);
  - session timeouts and a graceful Disconnect (protocol 2);
  - a headless **Server** build (`config=server_linux_debug`: no window, bgfx Noop, no ImGui);
  - `--server` / `--connect` / `--net-*` on the command line, with a JSON report;
  - `Tools/RunNetSmoke.py` in RunChecks.

- **NET-6:** networking from C#:
  - `[Replicated]` / `[Predicted]` script fields and `[ServerRpc]` / `[ClientRpc]` methods, generated allocation-free by the NetGenerator;
  - the `Net` API: role, commands, ownership, prediction stats;
  - networked input actions;
  - script prediction with rollback and resim;
  - ownership that reaches clients;
  - the schema in the handshake.

The editor tooling (NET-7) comes next.

## How It Works

### Conditioned links

`NetConditions` sets one-way latency, ± jitter, loss, duplicates, and a reorder chance with its extra delay. Draws come from a seeded xorshift, so a seed gives the same link on every platform.

- **Outgoing only:** conditions apply to what that end sends, so wrap both ends for a two-way link.
- **Update every tick:** `Update( now )` releases packets as they come due.
- **Delivery resolution:** a test harness should deliver at about 1 ms resolution, not once a tick. Otherwise arrival rounds up to the tick and reads as extra latency.

### NetConnection

Each `SendPacket( now )` sends one packet (the **main** packet), plus, while a blob is on its way, packets of blob slices alone (below). The main packet carries:

- **Header:** protocol id `0x4D45`, a 16-bit sequence, and the latest sequence received plus 32 bits for the ones before it, so each packet acks 33. It also carries the **ack delay**: how long that latest packet waited before this ack, in 0.1 ms units.
- **Reliable messages:** new ones, plus any unacked for a little over a round trip (`1.25 × RTT + 2 × deviation`, at least 20 ms; 100 ms before the first estimate). They go oldest first, within a 1024-message window and the 1200-byte packet budget, and, after the first of them in a packet, they leave room for the largest unreliable message queued that tick (a snapshot, owner state), so a late joiner's burst of Spawns never starves the state that goes in this packet or not at all, while the head of the reliable stream still goes out every packet.
- **Unreliable messages:** the ones queued since the last packet. Each goes in this packet or is dropped.
- **The blob trailer** (protocol 8), when there's anything for it: blob cancels due, then blob slices in whatever room the messages left. An older peer stops reading at the end of the messages, and an older peer's packet ends in zero padding, which reads as no trailer, so the handshake still turns a mismatched version away with its reason.

On receive, the connection works through these steps:

1. **Validate:** a packet with the wrong protocol id or a malformed body is rejected whole, before anything is applied.
2. **Duplicates:** a sequence it has seen, or one older than the ack window, is dropped as a duplicate.
3. **Acks:** these settle the sent-packet ring. Each settled packet's reliable messages stop resending.
4. **Round trip:** taken from the newest acked packet, minus the ack delay, then smoothed (1/8 gain, deviation 1/4). A packet read long after this end last sent gives no sample: it sat waiting on a hitch here (a long frame, a breakpoint), and that wait isn't the link's. Otherwise one hitch would read as a round trip of its length and a deviation of a quarter of it. "Long" is 50 ms or three of this end's usual gaps between packets (smoothed, each gap counted up to 0.5 s), whichever is more: an end that sends seldom (a 5 fps client, a 10 Hz server) must keep sampling, or its first sample (deviation: half the round trip) would stand for good. (At 100 ms a 0.05 s client hitch got through: its sample took the deviation on a 20 ms link to 14 ms, and the slack target's jitter floor to 2 ticks for seconds; ME-313.) A client also gives none from packets read more than two of its usual gaps between reads (frames: `NetConnection::BeginRead`, smoothed) after the read before: a hitch inside a frame, after its read and before its send, sends just before the next read, so the last send says nothing; the packets that came meanwhile sat unread (an in-frame 0.05 s hitch took the floor to 2 that way).
5. **Loss:** a packet that leaves the 33-packet window unacked counts as lost (`PacketsLost`, a smoothed `PacketLoss`).
6. **Reliable delivery:** messages are delivered in id order exactly once. Early ones wait for the gap to fill.

Messages are at most 1024 bytes (`kMaxMessageBytes`); anything larger goes over the blob channel (below). A session logs (once a second) any message it had to drop for being larger, instead of silently never sending it. A packet with a malformed message (a length past the limit) is rejected whole. Sequences and message ids wrap at 16 bits (`NetSequenceGreater`). For the session above it, the connection says of the packet it just took whether it was the newest (`WasLastPacketNewest`: no later main packet had arrived; every main packet, a keep-alive too, moves that on, so it never wraps however long a link idles; a packet of slices alone never counts, since one sent right after a main packet and arriving first mustn't make that packet's messages old news) and what of this end's packets it acked (`GetLastPacketAck`: the newest main packet acked, so an ack of a packet of slices alone sent right after a lost main packet doesn't pass for that packet; `NetClient`'s resync confirmation relies on it).

### Blob channel (ME-323)

Large messages (the killcam's stream, later levels or mod data) go over a fragmented reliable channel in `NetConnection`, beside the reliable stream and never in its way.

- **Sending:** `SendBlob( data )` queues up to `kMaxBlobBytes` (4 MB) and returns its id (from 1, never 0). An empty one, or one past the cap, is refused: 0, nothing queued, `BlobsRefused`. The session has `NetServer::SendBlob( client, data )` and `NetClient::SendBlob( data )`, for accepted clients and a connected client only.
- **Slices:** a blob goes in `kBlobSliceBytes` (1024) slices, each naming its blob, the blob's size and its index (so any slice starts the reassembly), the last one shorter. Each is resent until a packet carrying it is acked, after the reliable stream's delay (`1.25 × RTT + 2 × deviation`) plus the remote's wait before acking (the round trip leaves it out; held at about its longest lately, so a 20 Hz peer's 50 ms isn't beaten by a resend), an overdue resend before any new slice. Four blobs go at once (`kMaxActiveBlobs`), taking turns slice by slice; the rest wait in order.
- **Receiving:** slices land in place and duplicates are dropped (`BlobSlicesDuplicate`). A blob is delivered whole once every slice is in (`ReceiveBlob`, or the session's `OnBlob( client, id, data )` / `OnBlob( id, data )`, drained in `Receive`). Blobs complete in any order: a small one isn't held behind a big one. A late slice of a blob already delivered or cancelled is ignored: every id below the oldest not done, and those done above it, are remembered. A receiver holds at most `kMaxIncomingBlobs` (8) at once and ignores ids more than 2²⁰ ahead (`BlobSlicesIgnored` counts those, a ninth blob and a slice whose blob size changed), and it remembers at most `kMaxIncomingFinished` (4096) ids cancelled before it saw a slice of them, so an accepted peer can't make it hold more than 32 MB of blobs. Incoming blobs can be turned off per connection (`SetAcceptIncomingBlobs`): a server's connection takes none until it accepts the client, so an address that never said Hello reserves nothing (its slices are dropped and counted). A client keeps a blob that arrives whole before the server's Welcome (the packet carrying it lost or overtaken) and hands it to `OnBlob` once connected.
- **Cancelling:** `CancelBlob( id )` stops one of this end's not known to be done (true while any slice is unacked; it can lose the race: the remote may already have it whole, its last acks on their way, and then delivers it all the same), and `CancelIncomingBlob( id )` drops one arriving and tells the sender to stop (a skipped killcam). Either way a small cancel rides the main packets until one carrying it is acked, and the far end drops what it has (`BlobsCancelled`, `BlobsCancelledByRemote`). A blob its sender cancelled keeps its slot until the remote has heard, so the remote never holds more than four of a peer's at once. Every id is told, even one cancelled before a slice went. Likewise slices on their way when the receiver refused a blob are acked and dropped, so its sender may count it `BlobsAcked` (every slice acked) before the refusal arrives.
- **Bandwidth (`NetBlobSettings`):** live traffic always goes first and is never held back: the main packet is built before any slice, and slices only take the room it leaves. Then packets of slices alone (up to 16 a `SendPacket`) while the share allows. Two token buckets, refilled at each `SendPacket` and holding two of this end's usual gaps between packets of their rate (35 ms to 250 ms, at least a packet or two, so a 20 Hz end spends its whole rate): `LinkBytesPerSecond` (128 KB/s), the whole link's budget, debited by every packet sent, main ones included, so blobs get what live traffic leaves of it; and `MinBytesPerSecond` (16 KB/s), debited by slices only, so a blob still moves when live traffic takes the whole budget. A slice may go while either bucket is positive. The snapshot budget (NET-13's `BytesPerSecond`, below) is separate and unchanged: blobs never take from it.
- **Progress:** `GetBlobProgress()` lists every blob on its way: this end's (queued too: `Started` false) with the bytes acked, then the remote's with the bytes received and the bytes from the start without a gap (`ContiguousBytes`, for a stream read as it arrives). `NetStats` counts blobs sent, acked, received, cancelled and refused, and slices, resends, duplicates, slice bytes each way and the packets of slices alone.
- **The ack window:** each packet acks 33. A peer sending slice packets much faster than the remote sends anything would outrun it (slices acked by no packet, resent for nothing), so the link budget should stay well under 33 of the remote's packets' worth: at 128 KB/s it's about 2 slice packets a tick at 60 Hz.


### Sessions (NET-2)

1. **Hello:** the client sends Hello with its `NetVersion` on the reliable stream. A server whose version differs answers **Reject** with what differs (`"version mismatch: build scene"`). Otherwise it answers **Welcome**: client id, tick rate, its tick (past the ticks it still owes, when it's catching up after a hitch). A rejected client is dropped once the Reject is acked, or after 2 s; a connection that never says hello is dropped after 10 s.
2. **Start tick:** on Welcome, the client starts its clock at `server tick + RTT in ticks + target slack`, its ticks the server's length. The server is half a round trip past that tick by then; the client runs half a round trip further ahead, plus the slack.
3. **Commands up:** each client tick stamps the local command (`NetCore::SetLocalCommand`, later the HK-5 sampler) with the clock's tick. Each client packet carries the newest unconfirmed commands, up to 16, delta-coded, so a lost packet's commands ride the next ones.
4. **Server ticks:** each tick, the server takes every client's command for that tick from its `NetCommandBuffer`. A missing command repeats the previous one (a miss), and the server never stalls. The ticks before the client's first command are idle, not misses: its clock starts a few ticks ahead.
   - **Capped repeats:** a quarter second of misses in a row (`NetServer::kDefaultCommandRepeatSeconds` at the tick rate: 15 ticks at 60 Hz, 8 at 30) repeats the last command whole. Past that, the repeat keeps its aim and view tick but lets go of every button and axis, so a client that froze, crashed, sat at a breakpoint or lost its link while holding move and fire stops moving and firing instead of running on until the timeout.
   - **Resetting:** the next command that arrives in time ends the run. `NetServer::SetMaxCommandRepeats` changes the cap for every client, now and later (0: neutral from the first miss); `NetCommandBuffer::GetRepeatRun` and `GetNeutralCount` show it working.
5. **Slack:** measured at each consume as `newest tick received − (tick + ticks owed)`, then smoothed (1/10, or taken at once after a jump of 4 or more). The newest tick counts commands that arrived too late to run, so a client whose clock is behind reads as behind (negative slack), not as silent. (A client whose first commands were all late used to leave the buffer empty and the reports silent about it: it stayed behind for good.) The ticks owed are a catching-up server's (below). Commands more than 256 ticks past the server's tick are dropped.
6. **Reports down:** each server packet reports to each client the tick, the newest command it has, that client's slack (14 bits within ±64 ticks; past that, a bit says so and it goes in whole ticks, 32 bits: protocol 7), how many of its commands the server has had to repeat so far (protocol 4), and how long it has waited for the client's next command: since a packet with a new one arrived, in ms (protocol 7).
7. **Clock control:** the client notes every report in order (confirmations, misses) and steers by the newest of each `Receive`: `NetClock` sets dilation to `−(slack − target) × 0.02`, clamped to ±5%. More than 8 ticks off, or behind (below), it jumps instead (a resync), unless the report is a stall's (below). Past ±64 ticks (a client paused for minutes, a server that lost seconds to a hitch) the slack comes in whole ticks, measured the same way.

**Protocol 7** (the clock fix): on the wire, `ServerState` gains a bit before the slack (clear: whole ticks in 32 bits instead of the 14 quantized) and, after the misses, the server's wait (a VarUInt, ms). What fields mean changed too: the newest counts late commands, the slack is measured against the caught-up tick, the server rewinds a client's buffer when its clock goes back, and Welcome's tick is past the ticks owed. A client of this protocol on an older server would misread it (a client behind would read every report as a stall and never resync), so the build hash, which folds the protocol in, keeps them apart.

**Frame hooks** (`Engine::Run`; all no-ops without a session):

| Hook | Where | Effect |
|------|-------|--------|
| `Net->Receive( now )` | After the frame's events | Packets in |
| `Net->BeginTick( owed )` | Start of each fixed step | The server takes commands; the client stamps its command. `owed`: the steps still due after this one (a server catching up after a hitch) |
| `Net->EndTick( now )` | After the physics step | The server reports, one packet per client |
| `Net->Flush( now )` | Before the frame limiter | The client's packet: at most about one a tick (each new command sends; otherwise a keep-alive after two ticks without one), however fast it draws |
| `GetClockScale()` | The fixed-step accumulator | Scaled by it: dilation without changing `FixedDeltaTime` |
| `KeepsBacklog()` | Frame clamp and backlog drop | A server catches up on up to `NetServer::kMaxCatchUpSeconds` (0.5 s; past the 0.25 s frame clamp) instead of dropping steps, while its steps run faster than real time |

**Stalls and outages:** the slack only says where the clock is while the server hears the client. When the uplink drops out or slows (a Wi-Fi scan), or the client itself hitches, the newest command the server has stops moving, and the slack sinks a tick per tick with the wait. Read as a clock error, that sent the client ahead in jumps (about 134 ticks after a 1.5 s outage), then back too far once the link returned, and the server ran commands a second stale. So the client checks each report first:

- **Stalled:** the report's wait (the server measures it: since a packet with a new command last arrived) is past the usual: one send interval (smoothed, at least a tick) plus 2 ticks plus twice the round trip's deviation, that last at most 2 ticks. (A latency spike inflates the deviation, and mustn't pass the wait it caused off as usual. The deviation is the one from before the `Receive`, for the same reason.) Measured by the server, the wait is exact whatever the link does both ways at once: a client estimating it from its own send times and the round trip read a two-way spike's held reports as fresh, and resynced on them.
- **A stalled report steers nothing:** no dilation, no resync. When commands get through again they land where they did before, so the slack is right at once.
- **Gaps and outages:** the misses in a gap (the server waiting for the client's next command) are held until it ends: the newest moves and the wait is short again. A gap a deeper buffer could have covered grows the target then (NET-14: a burst of loss, a latency spike). One no buffer covers is an **outage**: a wait past the usual plus `MaxSlack` ticks, or, when the reports from the gap were lost, more misses than that less the buffer it ran down first. An outage's misses don't grow the target: the repeats (capped at a quarter second) cover it, and a deeper buffer wouldn't have. So the target is never deeper during or after an outage, not even for a moment.
- **Quiet:** with no usable report for `QuietTicks` (10), the clock stops dilating (the last report is getting old) and the time doesn't count as clean for shrinking the target. That covers a dead downlink, which sends no reports at all.
- **Hitches:** a long frame or a breakpoint on the client is a stall too, as the server sees it, not a clock error (and an outage, past `MaxSlack`). The round trip samples it would spoil are skipped (above). The engine runs at most 8 steps a frame and drops the rest, so a hitch past 8 ticks and a frame (0.15 s at 60 Hz) comes back behind: its commands late, which the next report says (below, **Behind**).
- **Its own hitches don't grow the target (ME-313):** a shorter one, caught up in full, leaves a gap a deeper buffer would have covered, but the gap is the client's, not the link's, and buffering for it would hold every command that much longer from then on. The client knows when it hitched: a packet with commands going out more than two ticks past its usual interval (smoothed, at least a tick; once it has 20 samples, as it starts from 0) carries a burst of ticks made late. When the gap the burst ends is over, its misses on the burst's ticks (one a server tick at most: they're counted against the ticks between the gap's reports) are the hitch's, and don't grow the target; any others in it do. Most hitches come inside a frame (Update, Render, after the frame's ticks and before its send): the late packet then carries only that frame's ticks, and the ticks due during the stall come in the next frame's packet, so the burst stays open for the next frame's first ticks (as many as the time from the frame's read to its send covers). Until the server has heard all of a burst its reports are about the hitch's gap, not the clock (the one on the late frame's tick alone reads as the clock 7 ticks behind after 0.14 s, and jumped it): the client holds the clock through them, as through a stall. Back to back (stutter) the bursts come faster than the round trip, so the client keeps up to 8 unheard ones (adjoining ones join); keeping only the newest, a second hitch before the report on the first took the first's misses to the target (0.1 s then 0.05 s on a 50 ms link: to 6). A Debug client hitches like this whenever it instantiates prefabs (spawns, effects). On a 20 ms link 0.05 s used to take the target from 1 to 4, 0.1 s to 6 and 0.15–0.17 s to 8 (`MaxSlack`), easing back over 20 s and more: about 100 ms more input latency after one hitch.
- **Counting:** `NetClient::GetStallCount`, `GetStalledReports`, `GetOutageCount`, `GetHitchCount` and `GetHitchMisses`; the client's JSON report has `Stalls`, `Outages`, `Hitches` and `HitchMisses`, and the net debug panel shows the hitches.

**A server hitch:** a server that stalls (a Debug server loading a joiner's world, say) then runs the ticks it owes, 8 a frame (`Engine::Run`'s cap; a headless server draws a frame a tick), sending a report after each. Measured against the step it's on, the slack read as every client far ahead until it caught up: they jumped back, then forward again, with misses between. So the slack is measured against where the server will be once caught up: `newest − (tick + ticks owed)`, the owed steps coming from the engine's fixed-step accumulator (`NetCore::BeginTick( owed )`). A client's clock kept time while the server didn't, and the reports say so.

- **How much:** `Engine::Run` lets a server (`KeepsBacklog`) past the 0.25 s frame clamp up to `NetServer::kMaxCatchUpSeconds` (0.5 s), keeps at most that much backlog, and `BeginTick` caps the ticks owed to it. A longer hitch loses the rest for good: every client reads its clock as that far ahead and resyncs back once (the server rewinds what they made past it).
- **Past ±64 ticks** (a hitch of about 1.5 s or more at 60 Hz) the slack goes in whole ticks, measured the same way. It used to be read as the newest command against the report's tick instead, which leaves out the ticks still owed; the report a client steers by comes from the first frame of the catch-up, with about 22 still owed. Clients jumped back that much too far: their commands came late through the 30-tick cooldown (neutral after 15 misses), the server ran commands they later made again, and a second resync went forward (a 3 s hitch: 2 resyncs, 50–57 misses, the target up to 8).
- **Very long hitches:** commands more than 256 ticks past the server's tick are dropped, so after a hitch that long (about 4.5 s at 60 Hz, 2.5 s at 128 Hz) the newest the server has falls short of the client's: the first jump back falls short and a second one follows after the cooldown (both back, no misses, nothing run the client took back).
- **A server that can't keep up** (its steps take longer than they cover, say a Debug build under load) drops its backlog instead of holding it: held, it would hold its clients' clocks ahead of what it simulates by the backlog, input latency for nothing. Its clients follow where it is, resyncing back each time they get 8 ticks ahead.

**Resyncs:** a report more than 8 ticks off target jumps the clock by the error, now only for a real offset: the link's latency changed by more than 8 ticks, the client came back behind from a hitch (the engine runs at most 8 steps a frame and drops the rest), or the server lost ticks to a hitch past 0.5 s. Each is logged on the client, `Net: clock resync N at server tick S: tick A -> B (slack X, arrived Y, target T[, back from a hitch])` (ME-323: so a test can tell a resync from a killcam's from one from the link or a hitch; HeroShooter's MatchSmoke fails on one while a killcam plays).

- **Behind:** a slack below −1 tick (`BehindTicks`) jumps to the target too, however close the target. Every command then arrives after its tick, each one a miss, and dilating back at 5% takes 20 ticks a tick: a client hitch of 0.18–0.25 s (8 steps caught up, 3–7 ticks behind) used to miss every tick on its return until those misses had grown the target (to 7 after 0.18 s) and it resynced anyway, then ease back for 20 s or more. Both the smoothed slack and the slack the newest command arrived with (the newest against the report's tick, plus the server's wait in ticks) must say so: the smoothed one lags a gap's end, and sinks through a gap's first ticks before the wait marks the report stalled. The jump goes by the lesser of the two.
- **Back from its own hitch,** the first report to steer once the server has heard all of a hitch's burst jumps if the clock is behind at all (`NetClock::OnSlackReport`'s behind threshold 0 rather than `BehindTicks`). Just past the engine's catch-up (8 steps and a frame: 0.155–0.175 s at 60 Hz, by the frame's phase) a hitch drops under a tick and a half, which a 1-tick target leaves up to a tick behind: inside `BehindTicks`, so it dilated back over about 30 ticks with every command late, and those misses took the target to 7 after 0.17 s (before ME-313 they took it to 8, deep enough to resync anyway). A hitch caught up in full comes back at the target, and a link spike isn't the client's hitch, so neither jumps.
- **After a jump** the clock ignores reports for 30 ticks, and the client ignores them until one comes in a packet that acks the client's first packet with a command made since (`NetConnection::GetLastPacketAck`): before that the server hadn't heard the new clock, and they describe the old one. They confirm nothing (after a jump back, the newest they name is a tick the client has made again, whose new command must keep riding its packets), and their misses are the old clock's, so they don't grow the target.
- **The cooldown's misses:** the client stops ignoring reports about a round trip after a jump, but the clock can't steer until its 30 ticks are up (`NetClock::InCooldown`). Misses in between are the jump's (it landed short, or the clock is still off), not the buffer's: they don't grow the target either. (A client starting 35 ticks behind used to grow its target from 4 to 8 in its cooldown, and jump to 8.)
- **Forward:** the ticks it skips would reach the server as misses (repeats of the last command, and target growth). The client sends its last command for the last 15 of them instead, so the server reads an unbroken stream.
- **Back:** the client makes ticks it already sent again. Its next packet ends on a tick older than the newest the server has; the server drops what it had past that tick (made for a timeline the client left, `NetCommandBuffer::GetRewindCount`), and the commands made again replace the old ones. Only the newest packet speaks for the clock (`NetConnection::WasLastPacketNewest`): a reordered older one only fills gaps up to the newest tick. The server never runs a command the client took back.

**Adaptive slack (NET-14, the Overwatch loop):**
- **Start:** the target starts at 3 ticks.
- **Grows:** each command the server reports having repeated (a miss) raises the target by a tick, up to `MaxSlack` (8), counted when its gap ends. Not an outage's misses, nor a resync's, nor any in a resync's cooldown, nor the client's own hitch's (above). So repeated latency spikes (Wi-Fi background scans) cost misses once or twice, then the buffer covers them.
- **Shrinks:** after `ShrinkAfterSeconds` (3 s) with no misses, it eases down by a quarter tick a second, while reports come. The floor is `max(MinSlack, 2 × jitter in ticks)`, where jitter is the connection's RTT deviation.
- **Where it settles:** on a clean link the buffer holds about 1 to 1.5 ticks, so input latency is as low as the link allows. On a 40 ms link with 2% loss it holds about 2.5 ticks.
- **Turning it off:** `AdaptiveSlack = false` keeps the fixed 3-tick target.
- **The debug report:** `NetCore`'s client report shows `TargetSlack`.

**Late presses:** a command that arrives after its tick already ran on a repeat isn't simply dropped. Any button it pressed that the repeat didn't have is merged into the next simulated tick, for that tick only (`NetCommandBuffer::GetRecoveredPresses`). A tap arrives a tick or so late instead of never, and the client, which predicted it, sees at worst a small correction. A button the client held through the gap isn't a press, even where a capped repeat had let go of it.

**Ending (NET-5, protocol 2):**

- **Disconnect:** a leaving client (`NetClient::Disconnect`, `NetCore::Close`) or a closing server (`NetServer::Close`) sends **Disconnect** with a reason. It is unreliable, since the sender is going, so it goes in three packets of its own. The server drops that client on its next `Receive` and counts it (`GetDisconnectCount`); the client becomes `Disconnected`, and `GetDisconnectReason()` says why (`"left"`, `"server closed"`).
- **Timeouts:** a peer that sends nothing for the timeout (10 s, `SetTimeout`, `--net-timeout`) is gone.
  - The server drops a silent accepted client and counts it (`GetTimeoutCount`). It still says Disconnect (`"timed out"`) on that client's own connection, in case the client is alive after all.
  - The server only sends to addresses that said hello. Packets from a session it has dropped get no answer, so such a client times out instead of hanging on half-open.
  - A client stops at `State::TimedOut`, counted from its Hello first going out, including while still connecting to a server that never answers.
- **One session per `NetClient`:** `Connect` works once; reconnecting needs a new client. Client ids (1–255) are reused only once no accepted client holds them.
- **`OnClientRemoved`:** fires for either, before the client goes. Replication forgets that client's state there, and `NetCore` keeps a summary for its report.
- **Session events for gameplay:** `NetCore` fires a typed `NetSessionEvent` (`Source/Cores/NetCore.h`) for who came and went, so every listener hears every event: `Listen<NetSessionEvent>` in an `EventReceiver`, or `EventSubscription::Create<NetSessionEvent>`, and check `Source` against your world (several worlds can hold sessions in one process). `ScriptCore` is one such listener and hands its world's to C# (below).
  - **When:** on the main thread, once the `NetCore::Receive` or `Close` that noticed them has finished (never from inside the session's loops, so a listener may even `Close`). `Engine::Run` calls `Receive` before the frame's fixed steps and scripts.
  - **The server:** `ClientJoined` once replication has sent the new client the world, `ClientLeft` with `"left"`, `"timed out"` or `"closed"` (its own `Close`).
  - **A client:** `Connected` with its id, then `Disconnected` with `"left"` (its own `Close`), what the server said (`"server closed"`, `"timed out"`), `"timed out"` (the server went quiet) or `"rejected: <why>"`.
  - **Reasons from the other side** are read with `BitReader::ReadText`: anything that isn't UTF-8 arrives as U+FFFD, so a hostile server's goodbye can't break the JSON that carries it to scripts (whose dump also replaces rather than throws).
  - `Engine::Run` closes the session only after its loop ends, so `"closed"` and a client's own `"left"` reach C++ listeners still alive then, never scripts.

### Replication (NET-3)

**Ids:**

- **Static:** placed `NetIdentity` entities get NetIds 1..N on both sides, in GUID order. The handshake's scene hash covers exactly these entities, so the orders match.
- **Dynamic:** entities the server gives a `NetIdentity` during the session get ids from `0x10000`. A reliable **Spawn** `{NetId, owner, prefab, pose}` goes to every client; the client creates it from `Prefab`, or as a bare entity with a Transform. A `NetIdentity` without a `Prefab` of its own takes the asset the entity was instanced from (`World.Instantiate` / `CreateFromPrefab`), so a prefab spawned from C# replicates as that prefab. `NetIdentity` is a registered script component: C# has typed wrappers, `entity.AddComponent<NetIdentity>()` with `Prefab`, `Priority`, `AlwaysRelevant`, `NetId` and `Owner`, and `NetHitbox` with `Shape`, `Center`, `Radius`, `Height`, `HalfExtents`, `Occluder` (ME-312). `hitbox.Enabled = false` takes a hitbox out of shots from the server's next recorded tick (a dead pawn). A `NetIdentity` itself can't be turned off from a script (refused with a warning): replication would keep sending it while `BeginTick` stopped routing its owner's commands; deactivate or destroy the entity instead.
- **Despawn:** destroying a networked entity sends a reliable Despawn.
- **Late joiners:** they get Spawns for every dynamic entity, and Despawns for the static ones already destroyed.

**Snapshots:**

1. **Capture:** after each server tick, `CaptureTick` records every networked entity's `NetEntityState` (quantized world pose plus `NetSchema` bytes).
2. **Delta:** each client's packet carries a delta against the newest snapshot that client acked (`SnapshotAck`, sent every client packet), or the full state with no baseline. The server keeps the last 128 sent per client (`kSentHistory`, ~2 s at 60 Hz: the baseline a delta uses is acked half a round trip after it was sent, so a baseline older than the history means full state every tick for that client, as for a fresh join). Changed entities write only the parts that changed, gone ones are listed, and the 900-byte budget defers the rest; the "sent" copy records what the client will really have.
3. **Skip when idle:** a snapshot identical to the last one sent is skipped, with a keep-alive every 15 ticks. The next snapshot names the previous one sent, so a client that has it knows entities held still in between (it adds a hold sample) rather than gliding from an old pose.

**Clients:**

- **Baselines:** decoded snapshots are kept (the last 128, `kReceivedHistory`, matching the server's `kSentHistory`) as delta baselines: the server's delta against the tick the client last acked arrives about a round trip after that tick, so both histories have to outlast a round trip (~2 s at 60 Hz; the old 32 covered ~530 ms, past which every snapshot was full state).
- **Samples:** each entity gets a sample buffer.
- **Render tick:** `WriteInterpolated` (after physics poses) shows each entity at render tick = `newest server tick − delay`. It's steered gently and snaps when more than 6 ticks off.
- **Interpolation:** linear for position, slerp for rotation, holding past the last sample. Replicated fields apply from the sample at or before the render tick.
- **Adaptive delay:** it grows by 0.25 tick when a moving entity runs out of samples (up to 8), and shrinks slowly after 2 s without that (down to 1.5).
- **Kinematic remotes:** a client's copy of a networked `Rigidbody` is made kinematic; only the server simulates.

**`NetSchema` field types:** bool, ints and enums (VarUInt), float, double, Vector2/3/4, Quaternion (raw floats), and strings. Flag fields with `ME_FIELD( X ).Replicated()`. `.Predicted()` implies Replicated. Script fields ride separately, after the reflected ones (NET-6, below).

### Relevancy and bandwidth (NET-13)

`NetReplicationServer::Settings` (`NetReplicationSettings`) shares out each client's bandwidth.

**Every tick, for every client:**

1. **Viewer:** `SetViewer( client, position )`, else the first entity the client owns.
2. **View:** entities within `RelevantRadius` of the viewer, plus `NetIdentity::AlwaysRelevant` ones and the client's own. An entity that leaves the view is listed as gone in that client's snapshots and holds its last pose there; when it comes back, it's sent whole.
3. **Accumulate:** each changed entity in the view adds to its accumulator: `Priority × (1 within NearDistance, else NearDistance / distance)`.
4. **Budget:** the packet's 900 bytes, and with `BytesPerSecond`, a token bucket of `BytesPerSecond / tick rate` a tick, holding up to two ticks' worth.
5. **Select:** highest accumulator first, each sized from what changed, while it fits.
6. **Encode:** the snapshot writes the selected entities. Their accumulators reset; the rest keep growing, so nothing starves.
7. **Jobs:** each client's snapshot is encoded on the job system (`UseJobs`); the packets go out on the main thread. Encoding reads only the shared capture and the client's own state, so the bytes match a serial encode.

Blobs (ME-323) have their own share of the link (`NetBlobSettings`, above) and never take from the snapshot budget.

### Prediction (NET-4)

The client predicts only what it owns. Remote entities are interpolated (above), and rigidbodies simulate only on the server.

- **Command handler:** `NetCore::SetCommandHandler( fn( Entity&, InputCommand ) )` is the same function on both sides. On the server it runs at `BeginTick` for every entity a client owns (`NetIdentity::SetOwner`), with that client's command. On the client it runs for the predicted pawn (`NetCore::Predict( pawn )`), with the local command quantized exactly as the server receives it.
- **Recording:** after each client tick, `EndTick` records `{command, CharacterController::NetState}`.
- **OwnerState:** each server packet carries, for each character the client owns, its full-precision mover state after the tick.
- **Reconcile:** compares that state with the prediction for the same tick: position within 1 mm, velocity within 1 cm/s, grounded equal.
  - **On a mismatch:** restore the server's state, then for every later recorded tick apply its command and run `PhysicsCore::StepCharacter`. Only the pawn re-runs; the rest of the world stays in the present.
  - **The visual jump** (where it was drawn minus where it now is) becomes `GetVisualOffset()`, which shrinks linearly to zero over 100 ms. A jump longer than `MaxSmoothDistance` (2 m: the server teleported the pawn, a respawn) snaps instead: the offset stays zero, so the pawn doesn't glide across the level.
  - **Before the first match**, a mismatch is the initial sync, not a correction: the server simulated idle ticks before the client's first command arrived.
  - **Presentation (NET-7):** the engine draws the pawn at its offset. `Engine::Run` calls `NetCore::BeginPresentation` once gameplay is done with the frame (after the late update) and `EndPresentation` after Render. Begin moves the pawn's Transform by the offset, so its children (meshes, an attached camera, audio sources), audio, the cameras' snapshot and render prep all see the drawn pose; End puts the simulated local position back bit for bit (unless something moved the Transform meanwhile: the editor's move stays). `Receive`, `BeginTick` and `Shutdown` end a presentation left open, so physics (which would read a moved Transform as a teleport), the network and scripts never see the offset. A script placing something from the pawn's pose in its own update (a camera that isn't the pawn's child, an effect) adds `Net.VisualOffset( pawn )` itself, as HeroShooter's `HeroPawn.ViewPosition` does. The Kit's camera rigs (GAME-1 `VirtualCamera`: Follow, Orbit's pivot and look-at, Platformer2D, a look-at target) do it for their target, so a rig on a predicted pawn glides with it instead of snapping the full correction.
- **Interpolation:** the predicted pawn is excluded from snapshot interpolation.

**The misprediction timeline (NET-7):** the predictor keeps the last 10 s of reconciled ticks (`kTimelineSeconds`, 600 at 60 Hz) and what each mismatch found, for the debugger.

- **Outcomes:** `GetOutcome( tick )` is `Match`, `Correction`, `InitialSync`, or `None` (not reconciled, or older than the timeline). `GetNewestReconciled` is where the timeline ends.
- **Records:** each mismatch on the timeline keeps a `NetMispredictRecord` (`GetMispredicts`, oldest first; `FindMispredict( tick )`). A record leaves with its tick: every reconciled tick, match or not, drops the records 600 ticks older, so after a quiet stretch the list is empty. At most 64 are kept (`kMaxMispredicts`): under a steady desync only the newest have a record, and `GetOutcome` still names the older ones as corrections. A record holds:
  - `Predicted` against `Authoritative` (the full mover state), `PositionDelta` / `VelocityDelta`, and `Describe()`: "position off 130.1 mm (0.0998, 0.0833, 0.0005), velocity off 7.803 m/s (...)", plus grounded, ground normal and script byte ranges when they differ.
  - The pawn's `[Predicted]` script bytes both ways and the `[begin, end)` ranges that differ (`ScriptDiffs`, from `NetPredictor::DiffBytes`).
  - What it takes to run it again: `Start` (the prediction after the tick before, with its script bytes and `PreviousCommand`; `HasStart` is false when that tick had left the history), `Commands` (the tick's own, then each later one the correction resimulated, up to `NewestTick`), and `Resimulated` (the state after each of those).
  - `PresentJump`: how far the correction moved the pawn's newest state (what the visual offset hides), and `ResimMilliseconds`.
- **Export and offline repro (`NetMispredict`, in `NetPrediction.h`):** `ToJson` / `FromJson` write a record at full precision (floats round-trip exactly; NaN and infinities, which JSON can't hold, as the strings `"nan"`, `"inf"`, `"-inf"`, so a blown-up mover comes back as it was; script bytes in hex; a field of the wrong type, as in a hand-edited file, makes `FromJson` / `Load` return false with the reason, and a `null` number reads as its default), `Save` / `Load` to a file, and `Export( record, path )` saves it on the background lane (a `Mispredict Export` task: no file write in a frame). `DefaultPath( folder, tick )` is `<folder>/Mispredict_<tick>.json`; the Net Debugger and the console write to the project's `.tmp/Net/`. `NetMispredict::Run( physics, pawn, record, handler )` runs it again through `PhysicsCore::StepCharacter` on a pawn in a world with the same level: from `Start` with the tick's command (the client's prediction again: `PredictedMatches`), then from `Authoritative` with the later commands (the resim again: `ResimMatches`). Only the mover: `[Predicted]` script state is in the file but isn't re-run.

**The Net Debugger (NET-7, `NetLaunch::DrawNetDebugger`):** a Player window for a client session in game-tools builds with ImGui, opened by `--net-debugger` or the console's `net debugger`:

- **Link:** client id and tick, RTT (and its deviation), loss, kbps in and out (the connection's byte counts over the last second), slack and its target, dilation, resyncs and hitches.
- **Blobs (ME-323):** received, sent and acked, cancelled (and by the server), slices sent, resent and duplicate, and a progress bar for each blob on its way either way (bytes so far of its size, queued ones marked).
- **Prediction:** corrections, matches, initial syncs, the last resim (ticks and ms) and the visual offset now (mm).
- **Timeline:** the last 10 s of reconciled ticks, one column each, drawn from `GetOutcome`: green match, red correction, grey initial sync, dark not reconciled. The mismatches with a record are drawn wider on top; hovering shows the tick's outcome (and says when a correction has no record left), or the mismatch under the mouse with its description; clicking a mismatch picks it (yellow).
- **The mismatch** (the one picked, else the newest): predicted against server position, velocity and ground normal with each delta (position in mm), grounded, the differing `[Predicted]` script byte ranges, the ticks resimulated and how far the present moved, its commands (tick, buttons, axes), and **Export**, which writes it to `.tmp/Net/Mispredict_<tick>.json` under the project on the background lane.
- **Console:** `net` (`net status`: the link, each blob on its way and prediction in a line), `net debugger [on|off]`, `net mispredicts` (how many mismatches the timeline holds and how many still have a record, each of those described) and `net export [tick]` (the newest, or that tick's).
- **Not in the replay viewer:** a match replay is the server's record, and a server doesn't know what its clients predicted, so client corrections aren't on the viewer's timeline (the replay's resim check finds the ticks a correction would have come at from the recorded movers instead).

HeroShooter: `Tools/MatchSmoke.py --client-args=--net-debugger` passes with the window drawn on both headless clients every frame (9 kills, Bravo wins 3-1), and their reports list the mismatches of the match's last 10 s: Alpha's 2 (a 10 m respawn with the `[Predicted]` bytes that changed, and a 14.1 mm, 0.845 m/s miss) of its 41 corrections, Bravo's 3 of 28.

**Not covered yet:**
- Only the pawn's mover and its C# `[Predicted]` fields roll back (NET-6). Other components on it don't.

### C# networking (NET-6)

A networked script is a `partial` class deriving from `Script`:

```csharp
public partial class Dasher : Script
{
    [Predicted] public float DashCooldown;     // the owner predicts it; rolled back and resimulated
    [Replicated] public int Health;            // server -> clients, in snapshots
    [ServerRpc] partial void RequestUse(Entity door);           // client (owner) -> server: OnRequestUse runs there
    [ClientRpc(NetTarget.Owner)] partial void HitMarker(Vector3 at);  // server -> owner (NetTarget.All: every client)

    void OnRequestUse(Entity door) { /* server */ }
    void OnHitMarker(Vector3 at) { /* client */ }

    public override void OnFixedUpdate(float dt)        // simulation: server, owner, and resim
    {
        var command = Net.Command(Entity);
        if (command.WasPressed("Dash") && DashCooldown <= 0) { DashCooldown = 2; /* ... */ }
    }
}
```

**The generator** (`Modules/ScriptCore/Generators/NetGenerator.cs`, beside CS-9's `SchemaGenerator`) gives the class `INetScript`:

- **State:** fixed-size, allocation-free `Save` / `Restore` of the `[Predicted]` fields, and `Write` / `Read` of the replicated ones.
  - The replicated block holds `[Replicated]` and `[Predicted]` fields, in declaration order, little-endian raw.
  - Read can skip the predicted ones, for the owner.
- **RPCs:** each send stub serializes its arguments into a stack buffer and calls `Net.SendRpc`. A dispatch switch calls `On{Name}`; RPC ids are the methods' ordinal name order.
- **Schema hash:** `__NetSchemaHash`, FNV-1a over the class name, every net field (name, kind, R/P) and every RPC (name, argument kinds, side, target).
- **Kinds:**
  - State: bool, int, float, double, int enums, Vector2, Vector3, Quaternion.
  - RPC arguments: those, plus string and `Entity`. An `Entity` is sent as its NetId.
- **Diagnostics** (errors; nothing is generated):
  - MEGEN004: not partial.
  - MEGEN005: not a `Script`.
  - MEGEN006: nested or generic.
  - MEGEN007: an unsupported, static or readonly net field.
  - MEGEN008: an RPC that isn't a bodiless `partial void`, or one named like an RPC on a base script.
  - MEGEN009: an unsupported RPC parameter.
  - MEGEN010: a missing or mismatched `On{Name}`.
- **Inheritance (ME-294):** a net script can derive from another (directly or through plain classes), and both layers can have net members.
  - **The interface:** only the root net script implements `INetScript`. Each layer generates `__Net*Layer` methods that a derived layer overrides, calling the base's first.
  - **Layout:** the base's state comes first, then the derived layer's appended.
  - **RPC ids:** a derived layer's ids start after the base's (`base.__NetRpcCount + i`), and its `On{Name}` handlers can stay private.
  - **Hash:** a derived `__NetSchemaHash` folds in its base's.
  - **Sealed:** a sealed root script gets private, non-virtual layers.
  - **No shared names:** a derived RPC can't reuse a base RPC's name (MEGEN008). MEGEN011, which used to reject the case, is retired.
- **Dispatch only runs on the side an RPC is addressed to:** a ServerRpc on the server, a ClientRpc on a client, both offline. A client can't make the server run a ClientRpc handler.

**`Net`** (`Modules/ScriptCore/Source/Net/Net.cs`, over the `## Networking` slots of `ScriptAPI.def`):

- **Session:** `Role` / `IsServer` / `IsClient` / `IsActive`, `LocalClientId`, `Tick`, `IsResimulating`, `Clients`.
- **Ownership:** `GetOwner` / `SetOwner` (server) / `IsOwner` / `IsPredicted`.
- **Input and stats:** `Command(entity)`, `LocalCommand`, `Press(action, held)`, `ButtonIndex(action)` / `AxisIndex(action)`, `Corrections` / `Matches` / `InitialSyncs`, `GetNetId` / `FindEntity`, `VisualOffset(pawn)`.
  - **`NetCommand`** reads buttons by name (`IsDown` / `WasPressed` / `WasReleased`) and axes by name (`Vector2("Move")`, `Value("Throttle")`; zero for an action not in the layout), or by index (`Axis(i)`).
  - **Building commands** (bots, tests): `Buttons |= 1 << Net.ButtonIndex("Fire")`, `Axis0`.. from `Net.AxisIndex("Move")` (a Vector2 takes two axes in a row), `PreviousButtons` = the last tick's, `Valid` and `HasAim` + `Yaw` / `Pitch`. The layout is the same on every peer, offline too.
- **`Net.LocalCommand`:** offline (no session; the Player, the editor's Play), the engine samples the networked actions into it every fixed step, before the scripts' `OnFixedUpdate`, exactly as a client samples its commands: `Net.Press` and the `Net.SetAim` aim included, axes quantized as the server would read them, `Tick` counting the steps. `PreviousButtons` is the step before, so `WasPressed` is an edge. `Valid` while any action is networked (or `Press` / `SetAim` holds something); a step that samples nothing clears it, so it never reads stale. The editor's Stop releases `Press` holds and the aim (`SingletonInput::ResetLocalInput`), so the next Play starts with none. So a pawn simulates from one command type online and offline: `var c = Net.IsActive ? Net.Command(Entity) : Net.LocalCommand;`.
  - **On a client:** its predicted pawn's command (a replayed tick's during a resim), or before it has one, the command it sends.
  - **On the server:** `Valid = false`; it has no local player.
- **Session events (EventBus):** `NetClientJoined(ClientId)` and `NetClientLeft(ClientId, Reason)` on the server, `NetConnected(ClientId)` and `NetDisconnected(Reason)` on a client (`ScriptCore` listens to `NetSessionEvent` for its world and queues them with the other engine events, delivered before the frame's `OnUpdate`). The server's own shutdown (`"closed"`) and a client's own quit (`"left"`) raise none: scripts have stopped by then. Subscribe with `On<NetClientJoined>(e => ...)` in a `Script`. A server spawns what a new client plays with there and hands it over with `SetOwner`; a leaver's entities stay (owned by the server again) until the game destroys them.
- **`CharacterController.Position`:** the simulated capsule (`NetState`'s position plus `Center`) after the last step, a `Teleport` or a correction. Read it in `OnFixedUpdate` (eyes, muzzles, distances): it's the same on the server, the owner and in resims, while the Transform is the pose interpolated between steps.
- **Without a session**, everything reads as offline, and RPCs run locally: offline play is both sides.

**Engine side:**

- **Networked actions:** `InputAction.Networked` (JSON `"Networked": true`) marks actions that travel in commands.
  - The world's `SingletonInput` (ME-297, a world singleton: `World::Singleton<SingletonInput>()`) holds the layout and the local command. It sorts them by name: buttons take the command's bits in that order, Value / Vector2 actions its four axes.
  - The client samples them at the start of each fixed step, before `Net->BeginTick`. A button counts as held, or as pressed since the last step, so a tap shorter than a tick still reaches one command.
  - The engine samples only once something is networked. Otherwise a C++ game's own `NetCore::SetLocalCommand` stands.
  - Offline it samples the same way into `SingletonInput::RecordLocalCommand` (C#'s `Net.LocalCommand`; a client records its commands there too). Without a session the layout follows the action map's hot reloads (`SingletonInput::RefreshCommandLayout`, against `InputActionSystem::GetMapVersion`); `Net.ButtonIndex` / `AxisIndex` caches follow `SingletonInput::GetLayoutVersion`, which no two builds share in any world. A session's layout is fixed.
  - Per world: the engine samples into its game world's `SingletonInput`, and C#'s `Net` (`LocalCommand`, `Press`, `SetAim`, the indices) reads the script world's, so a second world (a killcam or replay world, a test) never sees another's input.
  - `WasPressed` compares with the previous tick's command, which also holds in resimulation.
  - `Net.Press` holds a button from a script (tests, bots), until it's released or the editor's Play stops.
  - The layout's hash is part of the handshake.
- **`Net.Command(entity)`:**
  - **Server:** the entity's owner's command for this tick (and the one before; `NetServer::Client::Previous`).
  - **Owner's client:** its predicted pawn's live command, or the replayed one during a resim.
  - **Otherwise:** `Valid = false`.
- **Who simulates:** scripts on networked entities run `OnFixedUpdate` on the server, and on a client only on its predicted pawn (`NetCore::SimulatesLocally`; the rest follows snapshots). Non-networked scripts run everywhere. `OnUpdate` / `OnLateUpdate` are presentation and run everywhere.
- **Ownership:**
  - `Net.SetOwner` on the server goes out as an **Owner** message. Static entities' owners only travel that way, and late joiners get the current ones.
  - A client that comes to own an entity with a `CharacterController` predicts it (`NetCore::Predict`).
  - Losing it stops the prediction, and the entity follows snapshots again.
  - When a client leaves, what it owned goes back to the server.
- **State on the wire** (`NetCore::ScriptGlue`, filled by `Scripting/ScriptNet.cpp` through the `ScriptBridge` net entry points):
  - **Snapshot fields:** `[u16 reflected size][NetSchema bytes][script bytes]`.
    - Remote copies read all of it.
    - The owner reads the replicated fields of its own pawn too, skipping `[Predicted]`.
  - **OwnerState:** carries the pawn's `[Predicted]` bytes after the mover.
- **Prediction:** `NetPredictor::ScriptHooks`.
  - Each tick's record keeps the `[Predicted]` bytes, which `Reconcile` compares exactly.
  - On a mismatch the server's bytes are restored with the mover. Every replayed tick then runs the pawn's `OnFixedUpdate` before its character step: a live tick's order.
    - During the replay, `Net.IsResimulating` is true and `Net.Tick` is the replayed tick.
    - Every replayed tick runs the pawn's script, whether or not it has `[Predicted]` state, since it may steer the mover from `Net.Command`.
- **RPCs:** a reliable **Rpc** message `{NetId, rpc id, bytes}`, at most 1 KB (arguments up to `Net.MaxRpcArgsBytes`, 1008).
  - An entity the server created this tick has no NetId until the tick ends: RPCs on it are dropped, with a warning. Send them a tick later, or use a `[Replicated]` field.
  - The server runs a client's RPC only on an entity that client owns.
  - `ClientRpc` goes to the owner or to every client.
- **Handshake:** `ScriptSchema` folds in the networked action layout and every networked class's `__NetSchemaHash`, so a client with other script fields or RPCs is rejected (`"scripts"`). Protocol 3.

**Rules of thumb:**

- Don't log, play sounds or send RPCs from `OnFixedUpdate` without checking `Net.IsResimulating`: a resim runs it again.
- **`Net.PredictedEvent(this, eventId, value)`** (ME-291) is the way to raise FX and sounds from `OnFixedUpdate`; they arrive in `Script.OnNetEvent(eventId, value)`.
  - **Offline:** it presents right away.
  - **The server:** it presents right away and sends a copy to every client under the reserved RPC id `0x3FFFFFFF`, carrying its tick.
  - **The predicting owner:** it presents once per (entity, tick, event) (`NetEventLedger`, about the last 256 ticks). Resims of that tick, and the server's copy when it arrives, are skipped.
  - **Other clients:** they present it when the server's copy arrives.
  - **Requirements:** the script must be networked (any net member) on a `NetIdentity`.
  - **Not yet:** a predicted event the server never produced isn't cancelled, and there's no per-frame FX budget or queue (the GDC review's deferred-FX idea).
  - **Tested by:** NetProbe's dash: "dash fx once" on the owner and "server dash fx #1" on the server, in RunNetSmoke and RunChecks. With `RunNetSmoke.py --observer`, a second, non-owning client logs "observer dash fx once". The owner waits for it before dashing (`Game.NetProbePeers` = 2 through `--user-dir`). Under heavy load the owner still showed the event once through 6 real corrections.
- Keep `[Predicted]` state deterministic: the same code on the same commands. It is compared byte for byte.
- One `ScriptComponent` per networked entity carries its net state.

### Lag compensation (NET-8)

- **View tick:** each client command carries `ViewTick`, the render tick of its interpolated view when the command was made. It goes on the wire as how far behind the command's tick it was, in 1/64 ticks.
- **Aim (NET-15, protocol 6):** a command can carry the player's absolute aim, `HasAim` + `Yaw` (degrees about +Y; 0 looks down +Z, 90 down +X; wrapped to [0, 360)) + `Pitch` (up from level, clamped to ±90). Each is 16 bits (yaw to 0.0055°), sent only when it changes: a held aim costs two bits a command.
  - **Why absolute:** the server aims a shot exactly where the client's view did, and a lost command can't drift it, unlike mouse deltas clamped into the ±1 axes.
  - **Sampling:** C#'s `Net.SetAim( yaw, pitch )` (the world's `SingletonInput`, until `Net.ClearAim`) puts it in every command, quantized as the server will read it, so the client predicts with the same value.
  - **Reading it:** `Net.Command( e ).Yaw` / `Pitch` / `AimDirection` on both sides. Aim is input, not state: a pawn script that turns or shoots from it in `OnFixedUpdate` predicts it with nothing to correct. Remote players see the turn through the replicated pose.
- **Recording:** after each server tick, `NetLagCompensation::Record` stores every `NetHitbox`'s world pose: a capsule along local Y (Height, Radius) or a box (HalfExtents), around a local Center.
- **The shot:** a server command handler calls `NetCore::LagCompensatedRaycast( origin, direction, range, command, shooter )`.
  1. The hitboxes are interpolated to the command's view tick.
  2. The ray is tested analytically against each, skipping the shooter and everything under it (a pawn's own head hitbox). Nothing in the physics world moves.
  3. A present-day `RaycastAll` finds occluders, ignoring entities with hitboxes, whose bodies are in the present, and the shooter's own colliders and its children's (its gun).
  4. A hit behind an occluder comes back `Occluded`.
- **The cap (NET-15 high ping):** a view older than `MaxRewindSeconds` (0.4 s, about 300 ms RTT plus the view's delay and slack) is judged at the cap (`Clamped`) rather than refused. A target isn't pulled back behind cover further than that, and a shot that misses there simply misses. Only a view tick in the future is `Rejected`.
  - **The client side:** NetCore tells the replication client the oldest render tick the server will still accept (`SetMinRenderTick`: the next command's tick less the cap, less two ticks).
  - **Extrapolation:** when the interpolated view would be older than that, the render tick moves forward, up to `MaxExtrapolationSeconds` (0.25 s) past the newest sample. Remote movers then dead-reckon from their last two samples (`IsExtrapolating`). The view tick sent with commands stays what was really shown.
  - **The 600 ms doctest:** no shot rejected, 71 of 75 judged at the cap, the shown target within about 0.2 m of the server's at the view tick.
  - **Impact FX:** C#'s `Net.PredictImpacts` is false on a client whose round trip is over `Net.PredictImpactsMaxRttMs` (220 ms): draw impacts from the server's confirmation there instead of the predicted hit.
- **Broadphase (NET-15):** `Record` keeps each hitbox's bounds swept over the rewind window. A shot only rewinds and tests the hitboxes whose swept box its ray crosses (`LagCompensatedHit::Tested`), with the same results as testing all of them. In a 200-hitbox doctest that's about 7 tested per shot. `UseBroadphase = false` turns it off for comparisons.
- **Moving occluders (NET-15):** a `NetHitbox` with `Occluder` on (a door, a platform, the payload; give it a shape covering the collider) is recorded like any hitbox. It blocks a shot where it was at the shooter's view tick, never takes the hit, and the shot ignores its present-day colliders. A door that closed after the shooter saw it open doesn't block, and one that was closed in their view does.
- **From C# (NET-15):** `Net.LagCompensatedRaycast( shooter, origin, direction, range )` returns a `NetHit` (`Hit`, `Entity`, `Point`, `Occluded`, `Rejected`, `Clamped`, `Rewound`).
  - **On the server:** it is `LagCompensatedRaycast` with the shooter's current command, so `Rewound` is set. A shooter without a command is judged at the newest recorded tick.
  - **On a client, or offline:** it is `NetCore::PresentRaycast`, the hitboxes where that peer shows them now. That is the predicted hit, for impact FX; only the server's counts.
  - **The shooter's own parts** never stop its shots on any of these paths: a hitbox or collider on the shooter or any entity under it is skipped, so a pawn's child "Head" hitbox doesn't block a shot from its eye.
  - **Also exposed:** `Net.Command(e).ViewTick`, `Net.RoundTripMs` (a client's) and `Net.RoundTripMsTo(clientId)` (the server's, per client).
- **Overlays:** with `DrawOverlays`, each shot draws the ray (green on a hit, red otherwise), the rewound hitboxes in yellow and the present ones in gray, for 2 s.

### Match replays (ME-316)

The server's ground truth for a whole match, for debugging after the fact: one record per simulated tick, with no relevancy filter. ME-202's file replays share the format; ME-323's killcams are cut from the recorder's ring (below).

**A tick's record (`NetReplayTick`):**

- **State:** every networked entity's `NetEntityState` (the quantized pose and `NetSchema` bytes): `CaptureTick`'s snapshot, unfiltered.
- **Clients:** each client's command for the tick (buttons, axes, aim, `ViewTick`), whether it was a repeat (`Missed`), and its link: RTT (to 0.1 ms), loss (0.01%), command slack (0.01 tick), misses, late commands and clock resyncs so far.
- **Ticks:** the ticks still owed after it (a server catching up) and the backlog the engine dropped before it.
- **Entities:** what each NetId is (name, prefab, owner), when it appears or changes owner.
- **Movers (format 3):** every networked character's `CharacterController::NetState` after the tick (position, velocity, move input, pending move, ground normal, jump, grounded) at full precision, and its `[Predicted]` script bytes: what `OwnerState` sends the owner to restore from, so a resimulation can seed from any tick (the snapshot's pose is quantized to 1/1024 m and has no velocity).
- **Shots:** every lag-compensated shot: shooter and its client, ray, range, view tick, ticks rewound, hit / occluded / clamped / rejected, the hitbox hit (NetId and part) and point, and every hitbox as it was rewound. A projectile's per-tick sweep also names its **instigator** (who fired it, format 3) and counts as that player's client, not a bot's.
- **Events:** name, payload (free text, JSON by convention) and the NetId it's about: joins and leaves, and the game's own.

**The file (`.mreplay`, little-endian, versioned by `NetReplayFormat::kVersion`, 3):** readers take `kMinVersion` (2) to `kVersion`, so replays kept from before a format change still open; a chunk decodes with its file's version (`NetReplayChunk::Decode`'s `InVersion`). Format 2 has no movers or instigators.

1. **Header:** `MREPLAY\0`, the format version, then product, engine and game commits, map, command line, UTC time, tick rate, keyframe interval, net protocol, and the build, script schema, scene and `NetSchema::Hash` hashes (the reflected Replicated fields' layout).
2. **Chunks**, each self-contained: `MRCK`, first and last tick, tick count, codec, raw and stored size, an FNV-1a checksum of the stored bytes, then the ticks. The first tick's state is whole (the keyframe) and the rest are `NetSnapshotCodec` deltas against the tick before; a held command costs a bit. A mover writes only the vectors that changed, their float bits XORed with the tick before's, and its `[Predicted]` bytes XORed the same way (lossless; the unchanged high bytes come out as zeros for deflate). A shot whose hitboxes are the same as an earlier shot's in the tick (a pellet, a pierce step, arrows in flight) points at it instead of repeating them. The first tick also names every live NetId, so a chunk decodes alone: seeking decodes one chunk, and the same bytes can go over a network stream.
3. **Index:** `MRIX` and each chunk's offset and ticks, then the index's offset and `MRINDEX\0`. A file whose server died has none: readers scan the chunks and stop at the first torn one. While recording, the open chunk is also written after the whole ones every `FlushSeconds` (1 s) as a **tail**: an ordinary chunk with the ticks so far, overwritten by the next tail or by the whole chunk (bytes a longer tail left are cut off). A server that segfaults, asserts or is killed loses about a second, not up to a chunk (5 s); only a crash in the middle of overwriting a tail loses the open chunk.

**Size** (HeroShooter's MatchSmoke, Debug, 2 players, 6–16 networked entities, 117 s): 843 KB, about 433 KB a minute; 2.9 MB before deflate. Format 2, without the movers, was 548 KB (280 KB a minute; 1.86 MB before deflate): two pawns' movers cost about 150 KB a minute (491 KB a minute before their vectors and `[Predicted]` bytes were XORed against the tick before). With 2 bots too (4 pawns, 33 kills, 1092 shots, 136 s): 1965 KB, 868 KB a minute, before the XOR. In format 2: The states are most of it (two pawns' ~110 bytes of `[Replicated]` script fields change every tick and go whole), then the commands (the autopilot turns every tick). Re-encoding the same match at other keyframe intervals: 30 ticks 858 KB, 60 770, 150 709, 300 689, 600 679, 3600 669 (before the link-stat change below), so 300 ticks (5 s) costs 3% over one keyframe for the whole match while seeking decodes at most 300 ticks and a chunk is ~25 KB (a killcam stream's slice). The link stats are kept at their resolution (RTT 0.1 ms, loss 0.01%, slack 0.01 tick) and sent only when they change, and axes, yaw and pitch go at the wire's 16 bits when they're on its grid (raw floats otherwise): that took the match from 689 KB to 548.

**Recording (`NetReplayRecorder`):** `Record` keeps a ring of the newest ticks (`RingSeconds`, 12 s) and every `KeyframeInterval` ticks (300: 5 s at 60 Hz) hands the chunk to a `Jobs::SerialQueue` ("Replay Writer") on the background lane (ME-319), which encodes, deflates (zlib) and writes it in order. The tick never waits on the disk; `Close` writes the last chunk and the index and waits, up to `CloseTimeoutSeconds` (30 s: past it a hung disk is given up on, the file left without its index, so the shutdown's report and goodbyes still go out). A write that fails stops the file (logged once); the ring goes on. So does a disk that can't keep up: chunks waiting past `MaxBacklogSeconds` (60 s of the match) stop the file and are let go rather than held in memory without end (a warning comes first, at 8 waiting). Tails are only queued when the lane has caught up, so they never add to a backlog. A ring-only recorder (path `""`) names every live NetId at the same keyframe ticks as a file would, and the others carry only what changed; `GetEntities()` is the table as of the newest tick.

**On the server (`NetCore`):** `StartRecording( path, header, settings )` (a server only; path `""` keeps the ring alone) fills the header's tick rate, protocol and hashes from the session and records every tick in `EndTick`, straight after `CaptureTick`:

- each accepted client's `Current` command, `Missed` when its miss count moved, and its link stats;
- the ticks owed (`BeginTick`) and those `Engine::Run` dropped since the last tick (`NoteDroppedTicks`: past `kMaxCatchUpSeconds` of backlog, or a backlog it can't run);
- a NetId's name, prefab and owner the first tick it's seen and whenever its owner changes;
- every networked entity with a `CharacterController`: its `GetNetState()` and `NetScriptState::SavePredicted` bytes;
- every `LagCompensatedRaycast` of the tick (C#'s `Net.LagCompensatedRaycast` on the server goes through it, with or without a command: a bot's is judged at the newest tick, client 0), with the hitboxes at the tick it was judged at, the shooter's own left out; a hitbox on a child (a Head) names its nearest networked ancestor. Its `InInstigator` (C#: `Net.LagCompensatedRaycast( projectile, origin, direction, range, instigator )`, HeroShooter's arrows) names who fired a projectile's sweep, and the shot's client is the instigator's owner;
- `net.joined` / `net.left` events (`{"client":N}`, with the `reason` for a leave; the clients already there when recording starts count as joined), and `RecordReplayEvent( name, payload, netId )`'s.

**From C#** (`Modules/ScriptCore/Source/Net/Replay.cs`): `Replay.Event( name, payload )` and `Replay.Event( name, payload, entity )` add the game's own moments (kills, picks, damage, respawns) at the current tick, `entity`'s NetId with them (what `--entity` filters on; looked up when the tick is recorded, after `CaptureTick`, so a pawn instantiated that tick is named too); `Replay.IsRecording` says whether this process records; `Replay.Quote( text )` makes a JSON string literal for a payload. Off the recording server (a client, offline, a server without `--record-replay`) they do nothing, so gameplay calls them unconditionally.

`StopRecording` (and `Shutdown`) writes the rest and logs `Replay: wrote <path>: N ticks (S s) in C chunks, X KB (Y KB before deflate)`. The server's `--net-report` gains a `Replay` block (path, ticks, chunks, bytes, raw bytes, error).

**Reading:** an `INetReplaySource` gives the header and chunks (`NetReplayFileSource` from a file, `NetReplayMemorySource` from bytes, which a killcam's chunks fill as they arrive). `NetReplayReader` reads any tick (decoding its chunk from the keyframe, kept for the next read) or a range in order.

**ReplayDump** (`NetReplayDump.h`): any game executable (the Player, the headless Server Player, a game's own) given `--replay-dump <file>` prints the replay and exits, before the project, window or scripts start (`ME_APPLICATION_MAIN`); in the dev tree, run it through `Tools/NixEnv.sh`.

| Option | Prints |
|--------|--------|
| (none of the below) | the summary, events, shots, and each client's command when its input changes or it missed one |
| `--summary` | the header (product, time, map, commits, command line, tick rate, hashes), ticks and seconds, most ticks owed and ticks dropped, each entity (NetId, name, its owners in order, e.g. `1>0` when its client left, prefab), each client (ticks, RTT average / max, loss, misses, late commands, resyncs), event counts by name, shots hit / missed / occluded / clamped / rejected (trigger pulls), and projectile sweeps and their hits apart |
| `--events` | `<tick> event <name> <payload> [<entity>#<id>]` |
| `--shots` | `<tick> shot client N <shooter> [(sweep, fired by <instigator>)] HIT <target> (<part>) at (x, y, z) d=… from … dir … view … rewound … ticks`, a `MISS` (or `MISS (occluded …)`), then each rewound hitbox |
| `--commands` | every tick's command per client: buttons, axes, aim, view tick, `(missed: repeated)` |
| `--net` | per tick: owed and dropped ticks, each client's RTT, loss, slack, misses, late commands, resyncs |
| `--states` | per tick: each entity's position, rotation and field bytes (hex), and each character's `mover` at full precision (position to 6 decimals in text, exact in JSON's `movers`): velocity, input, grounded / airborne, jump, `[Predicted]` bytes |
| `--ticks a:b` | only ticks a to b (`a:` and `:b` work); names still come from the chunk's start |
| `--entity id` | only that NetId: its events, the shots it fired, instigated or took, its owner's commands, its states and mover |
| `--markers` | the viewer's timeline markers (below): `<tick> marker <kind> <label>` and the count of each kind over the whole replay; JSON: `markers: [{tick, kind, name, label, entity, client, ticks}]`. Alone it prints no tick sections |
| `--json` | one JSON document: `header` and `summary` (with `--summary`), `markers` (with `--markers`), and `ticks`: `[{tick, events, shots, commands, net, states, movers}]` with the ticks that have any; `summary.sweeps` counts projectile sweeps |

Exit codes: 0, 1 when the file can't be read, 2 for a bad option. A file without its index says so and reads up to its last whole chunk.

**Timeline (`NetReplayTimeline.h`, phase 3):** one pass over a replay gives the moments a viewer jumps to and each client's link along the match. Nothing in it needs the engine (ReplayDump's `--markers` prints it). Markers, in tick order:

| Kind | From |
|------|------|
| `kill` | an event named `kill` (the game's, `Replay.Event`); a payload with `killer` and `victim` strings labels it `kill <killer> > <victim>` |
| `join` / `leave` | `net.joined` / `net.left` (the client from the payload) |
| `event` | any other event (picks, damage, respawns) |
| `hit` | a lag-compensated trigger pull that hit (a projectile's sweep isn't one) |
| `miss` | a client's command hadn't arrived and the server repeated the last: where its prediction was most likely corrected (the server records no client corrections). Misses within 10 ticks of each other are one marker spanning them |
| `resync` | a client's clock went back (its command buffer's resync count rose) |
| `hitch` | the server was behind (ticks owed after a tick) or dropped backlog; within 30 ticks of each other, one marker |

Each client also gets a value per tick from its first (`NaN` while it wasn't connected): RTT, loss, command slack and whether its command was repeated; the timeline keeps the ticks owed and the trigger pulls per tick too. `Find( kind, n )` (n < 0 counts from the end) and `Neighbour( tick, forward, kinds )` look markers up.

**Resim check (`NetReplayResim.h`, phase 3):** does a character's recorded movement follow from its owner's recorded commands? From a tick's recorded mover (format 3: `CharacterController::NetState` at full precision and the `[Predicted]` bytes), each later tick is simulated again with the command the server ran for its owner, the way the owner's prediction resimulates (`NetPredictor`): the command handler, the predicted scripts' fixed step, then `PhysicsCore::StepCharacter`. The result is compared with that tick's recorded mover: position within 1 mm and velocity within 0.01 m/s (the predictor's tolerances), grounded exactly, `[Predicted]` bytes exactly. A divergent tick is one the owner's prediction would have been corrected at: the server did something the commands don't explain (damage, a shove, a respawn), or the simulation isn't deterministic. The check carries on from that tick's recorded mover, so every divergence in the range is found (`Result::Divergences`, the first 64, and `DivergenceCount`); ticks without the mover or the owner's command are gaps, and the next one with both starts again. `Run( reader, netId, from, to, hooks )` takes the engine side as hooks; `FindCharacters` lists the NetIds a check can run (a recorded mover and an owning client: a bot's commands aren't recorded); `Describe` is one line plus a line per divergence.

`NetCore::ResimulateReplay( netId, from, to )` runs it in the viewer's world while a replay plays: the character's own entity, the world's command handler, the script glue's `SimulatePredicted` with `IsResimulating` true, `GetCommand` and `GetTick` the recorded tick's and `IsPredicted` true for that entity (C#: `Net.IsResimulating`, `Net.Command`, `Net.Tick`), then `StepCharacter` against the world's colliders. Before each step the other characters in the world stand where the recording has them as that tick began (the tick before's movers, `Hooks::PoseOthers`), so their capsules are where the server had them, not where the tick shown has them; the character pushes no dynamic bodies (`PushStrength` 0 for the run). The rest of the world (props, doors) stays at the tick shown. It reads with a reader of its own (the playback keeps its chunk), and puts the character's mover and `[Predicted]` state and the others' movers and Transforms back afterwards. A range with nothing after its seed tick says so (`nothing to compare`), never `every one matches`.

**Playback (`NetReplayPlayback.h`, phase 2):** a source's ticks played into a world through the client's own replication path. Each tick becomes the messages a server would have sent for it, written by the server's own encoders (`NetReplicationMessages`: spawns with the recorded prefab, owner and pose, despawns, owner changes, then a `Snapshot` delta against the tick before), and goes into `NetReplicationClient::Deliver` as if it came off the wire. So prefab spawning, the snapshot codec, sample buffers, interpolation, `NetSchema::Read` and the C# `[Replicated]` fields all run as on a client. The viewer owns nothing, so nothing is predicted. `NetReplicationClient( world )` (no `NetClient`) is the session-less client it feeds; its render tick is driven (`DriveRenderTick`): no delay, steering or extrapolation, the world shows exactly the playhead's tick (between ticks, the two either side, interpolated).

- **Controls:** `Play` / `Pause`, `Step( ±N )` (pauses, whole ticks), `Seek( tick )`, `SetSpeed` (clamped to 0.1×–4×), `SetLoop( from, to )` (past `to` it jumps back to `from`), `SetPauseAt( tick )` (playing onto or past it stops there, once). The end of the replay pauses on its last tick. `Update( frameSeconds )` moves the playhead, then feeds what showing it takes, before the client's `Update`.
- **Feeding:** forward by up to 48 ticks (a fast-forward, a short jump) it feeds the ticks in between, each a delta. Back, or further, it **rebuilds**: `ResetHistory` on the client, the entity table from the tick's chunk keyframe (`NetReplayReader::GetKeyframeTick`) to the tick, despawns for what the tick doesn't have, spawns for what it has and the client doesn't, owners, then the tick whole. On a whole tick only that tick is fed (the next one's spawns and despawns would change the world a tick early); between ticks, the next one too.
- **Placed entities** a server destroyed are destroyed in the viewer's world too, as on a client. Seeking back before that can't bring them back (logged once; reload the replay).
- **Check (`CheckWorld`):** at a whole tick, every recorded entity is in the world, the sample the client showed is the recorded `NetEntityState` bit for bit, and the world re-captured the server's way (`NetReplicationState::Capture`: pose quantized, `NetSchema` bytes, script bytes) has the same position and fields exactly and the same rotation within 0.002 rad (decoding and re-encoding the smallest three can land a step over); nothing in the world the recording doesn't have.
- No engine globals: the source, the client and the world are the caller's, so a replay world of its own (ME-297, the killcam) is a move.

**In `NetCore`:** `StartPlayback( source )` plays a replay into the core's world with no session. It refuses while a session or playback runs, an empty replay, and a replay recorded on another scene (the header's scene hash against this world's placed `NetIdentity` entities: the static NetIds wouldn't line up); another `NetSchema` or script schema only warns. Then `IsActive`, `IsClient` and `IsPlayback` are true (`IsServer`, `IsSessionLive` and `GetClient` aren't): the viewer is a client that **presents**. `SimulatesLocally` is false for every networked entity, so their fixed-step scripts don't run and the recorded `[Replicated]` fields drive them; nothing is owned, predicted or commanded (`GetCommand` is false), owner changes only reach `NetIdentity`. Non-networked entities and scripts run as ever. `WriteInterpolated` moves the playhead and poses the world; `GetTick` (C#'s `Net.Tick`) is the tick shown; `CheckPlayback` is `CheckWorld` with the script glue's bytes; `WriteReport` says `"Role": "replay"`; `Shutdown` ends it. **C#:** `Replay.IsPlaying` and `Replay.Tick` (-1 when none plays): a game hides its "connecting" UI and its own player's view when playing (`Net.LocalClientId` is 0).

**The viewer (`Source/Engine/ReplayViewer.h`, phase 3):** NetLaunch's playback gets a `ReplayViewer`, which keeps a file source and reader of its own (the playback keeps its decoded chunk) and gives the debugging tools:

- **Timeline:** built on the background lane when the replay opens (a `Replay Timeline` task, its own file source), logged as `Replay: timeline: kill 10, join 2, ...`. Game-tools builds draw it under the Replay window's controls as a scrubber: each kind's markers in its colour and height (kills, joins and leaves tallest), the playhead, the loop; under it a strip per client with its RTT (green, against its highest), command slack (blue) and repeated commands (red). Hovering shows the tick, each client's link there and the markers under the mouse; clicking a marker jumps to it (the tallest kind under the mouse), clicking or dragging elsewhere seeks. Checkboxes pick the kinds shown, which `[` / `]` and the `< marker` / `marker >` buttons visit. A jump shows the tick before first, then steps onto the marker's tick, so the inspector can say what changed; it selects the marker's entity.
- **Inspector** (the Replay Inspector window, the report's `entities`): every entity recorded at the tick, the changed ones marked; for the selected one its recorded pose, its C++ Replicated fields by name (`NetSchema::Describe` of the state's NetSchema part against the entity's components: a state's `fields` are `[u16 C++ size][C++ bytes][script bytes]`, split by `NetReplicationState::Unpack`), its C# script's public fields as the world has them at the tick (HP, ammo, cooldowns: the `[Replicated]` ones from the snapshot, the `[Predicted]` ones restored from the recorded mover), the recorded bytes and which changed, and a character's mover (position to 6 decimals, velocity, move input, grounded, `[Predicted]` bytes). Everything that changed since the tick before is in yellow; the script fields only when the world showed the tick before (a jump does). Follow, POV and resim buttons.
- **Input** (Replay Input, the report's `inputs`): each client's command this tick by the networked actions' names: buttons held (green) and pressed this tick (yellow), axes, aim, view tick, whether it was a repeat, and its link.
- **Lag compensation** (Replay Shots, the report's `shots`, and the world): each shot this tick with its ray (green hit, red miss, orange occluded) and hit point, the hitboxes as it was judged against them (rewound: yellow, the one hit red), the same hitboxes live in the world at the tick (`NetLagCompensation::Record` of the viewer's world: grey) joined to their rewound pose by a cyan line, and how far the rewind moved each (`rewindOffset`). One shot can be picked to draw alone.
- **World overlays** (`DebugDraw`, drawn over the scene): the shots, every live hitbox, and each character's recorded path over the last 2 s (cyan / magenta by owner).
- **Camera:** the game's own, free (WASD, Q / E down and up, Shift faster, right mouse to look), following an entity from behind (its owner's recorded aim, else its facing), or its POV: a player's eye and aim from its tick whose command's view tick is the tick shown (ME-343: what its screen had, as its killcams show it, through `KillcamPlayer::ReadKillerSamples` / `FindKillerView` from a reader of the viewer's own; a bot's from where it is and faces), at the game's eye height (`Net.Killcam.EyeHeight`) or near the top of its capsule, its own meshes, models and text hidden while it's the POV (its eye is ahead of its body by that delay). `NetLaunch::UpdateReplayCamera` places the current camera after the scripts' late update, before the cameras' snapshot.
- **Resim** (`NetCore::ResimulateReplay`): from the inspector (from the tick's keyframe to it, from the keyframe before when the tick is one, or the whole replay), the console, or `--resim-check`, which seeks to each player's character's first tick (so its entity and script are up), checks its whole span and logs `Resim: <name>#<id> client N ticks a-b: X resimulated from tick a (mover and [Predicted] state), G gap(s), D diverge; the first at tick T` and a line per divergence.
- **Report** (`--replay-report`, console `replay report`): `tick`, `beforeTick`, `marker` (the one jumped to), `markers` (counts) and `markersHere`, `entities` (`netId`, `name`, `owner`, `position`, `rotation`, `fields` hex, `poseChanged`, `changedBytes`, `replicated` [{component, field, value, changed}], `scriptBytes` (the fields after the size and the C++ bytes), `script` {name: value}, `scriptChanged`, `mover` {position, velocity, moveInput, grounded, predicted, predictedChanged}, `world`), `inputs` ({client, pawn, buttons, held, pressed, axes, yaw, pitch, viewTick, missed, link, text}), `shots` (the dump's fields plus each hitbox's `rewound`, `live` and `rewindOffset`), `events`, `resim` ({netId, ticks, gaps, divergenceCount, firstDivergence, divergences}) and the camera.
- The console's `replay` command gains `markers [kind]`, `marker <kind> [n]`, `next [kind]`, `prev [kind]`, `inspect [netid]`, `input`, `shots`, `resim [netid] [a:b]` (default: the inspector's character, from the tick's keyframe to the tick), `camera game|free|follow <id>|pov <id>`, `overlay shots|hitboxes|trails on|off` and `report <file>`.

While a replay plays, `NetCore` also restores each character's recorded `[Predicted]` script state at the tick shown (format 3 movers) every frame: snapshots don't carry it, and the scripts' presentation and the inspector read it.

**In the Player (`NetLaunch`):** `Player --replay file.mreplay` plays it (the options are in the command-line table above; the window title says `[replay]`). The console's `replay` command drives it: `replay` (status), `play`, `pause`, `toggle`, `step [n]`, `seek <tick>`, `speed <x>`, `loop <a> <b>` / `loop off`, `pause-at <tick>`, `check` (the problems, or `check passed`), `info` (the header). `--console "replay seek 609"` runs one at start-up (the first frame, after the replay starts), and an edscript's `console replay ...` reaches it wherever a replay plays. Game-tools builds draw a **Replay** window: play / pause, ±1 / ±10 ticks, speed (0.1–4×), a tick slider to scrub, the loop and its range, the time, and the tick's events and shots; Space plays / pauses, Left / Right step a tick (Shift: 10). HeroShooter: `Tools/MatchSmoke.py --record-replay` plays its replay back at a kill and checks it (below).

### Killcams (ME-323)

When a player dies they watch the last seconds as the killer saw them, the way the server had it, from a stream the server sends (never the victim's own interpolation buffer: half a second, filtered to the victim's view, without the killer's aim), played into a world of its own while the live one goes on (the Overwatch model). In one line each:

- **The ring** (ME-316's recorder): the server's last 12 s of every tick, unfiltered; servers keep it unless told `--no-replay-ring`.
- **The blob channel** (`NetConnection`, protocol 8, above): large reliable messages in 1 KB slices on a share of the link that never displaces live traffic.
- **The encoder** (`NetKillcam`, on the background lane): the ring's last seconds cut to the killer's view and aim, in ME-316 chunks, a blob each.
- **The receiver** (`NetKillcamReceiver`): the chunks back into a replay source as they arrive.
- **The replay world** (`KillcamPlayer`, ME-297): a second `World` the source plays into, presented instead of the live one from the killer's eye; `[ReplayWorld]` scripts present in it.
- **The API:** `Net.SendKillcam` on the server; `Net.Killcam` (`IsPlaying`, `Started` / `Ended`, `Skip()`, ...) on the client; the game's RmlUi HUD stays over it.

Phases: 1 the blob channel, 2 the ring and encoder, 3 the replay world and presentation, 4 HeroShooter's death cam (below, **In a game**).

**The ring:** the per-tick capture `NetReplayRecorder` already keeps (`RingSeconds`, 12 s: every entity's state with no relevancy filter, every client's command and link, the movers, shots and events). The `.mreplay` file is one reader of it and the killcam another: nothing is captured twice. A server started by `NetLaunch` keeps the ring alone (`StartRecording( "" )`, logged `Net: keeping a 12 s replay ring for killcams`) unless it records a file (`--record-replay`, which has the ring too) or is told `--no-replay-ring`. The recorder also keeps the NetId table as of the ring's oldest tick (`GetRingEntities`), so any stretch of the ring can be named from there.

**Sending (`NetCore::SendKillcam( victim, killer, seconds = 8 )`,** or `( client, victimNetId, killerNetId, seconds )`): the game decides what a kill is. On the server, with the ring on, for a victim a client owns (a bot has nobody to show it to) and a networked killer; the seconds are capped to the ring. The death is the current tick (the one being simulated, or the next one outside a tick). Once that tick is recorded (`EndTick`, straight after the replay's own record), the ring's ticks up to it and the table go to the background lane (`Killcam Encode`, ME-319), which filters and encodes them; the tick never waits. Then the chunks go a blob each over the victim's connection: the first alone, so it has the whole blob share and the victim can start on it, the rest at once when it's acked (the channel sends four at a time, the rest queued in order). A newer killcam for the same client cancels the older one's blobs; a client that leaves takes its killcam with it. Logged `Net: killcam N for client C: victim #v, killer #k, ticks a-b, X KB in N chunks (Y KB before deflate; encoded in Z ms)`.

**What the stream holds (`NetKillcam::Filter`):** the ticks `death - seconds + 1 .. death`, each cut to the killer's view:

- **State:** every entity the tick has, as the killer's client had it. In its view (NET-13's rule from the killer's pawn, its state's position that tick, or the nearest tick it has one: within `RelevantRadius`, `AlwaysRelevant`, owned by the killer's client, the killer itself, and the victim however far, the death it shows; the same `NetReplicationSettings::IsRelevant` the snapshots use): the state that tick. Out of it: the last state the killer's client had, as a client holds it (its last in view, or the one it first has in the ring: the spawn pose, for one spawned since), so the victim learns nothing the killer's client didn't. Spawns and despawns go to every client whatever its view, so an entity leaves the stream only when it's destroyed, and a playback never despawns one for leaving view (a placed one couldn't come back). `AlwaysRelevant` is every NetId that was so at a tick in the ring: `NetCore` keeps them as it records (pruned with the ring), so a projectile destroyed before the cut still counts.
- **Clients:** the killer's client's command (buttons, axes, aim, view tick) and link; none for a bot.
- **Movers:** the killer's (full precision and `[Predicted]` bytes), for its camera.
- **Shots:** those fired by, through (a projectile's instigator) or at an entity in view, with only the hitboxes in view (and the unnetworked ones).
- **Events:** those about an entity in view, or about none (a `kill` about the victim is always in).
- **Entities:** a chunk's first tick names everything in it, later ticks what spawns and owner changes, so every chunk decodes alone.

**The stream:** ME-316 chunks of `KillcamSettings::ChunkTicks` (60: 1 s), deflated like the file's, each blob `"MRKC"`, a version, the killcam's id (from 1, per server), the chunk's index and count and the stream's total bytes; the first also carries the victim, the killer, the killer's client, the death and first ticks, and the replay header (the recording's, without its command line; `KeyframeInterval` the chunk length).

**Receiving (`NetKillcamReceiver`):** `NetCore` on a client takes its session's blobs (`NetClient::OnBlob`). Chunks may complete in any order; they go into a `NetReplayMemorySource` (`GetKillcam().GetSource()`, made when the first chunk arrives) in order, so a `NetReplayReader` can read the first second while the rest stream. A newer id replaces the killcam; an older one's late blobs are stale; a blob that isn't a killcam's is ignored and a torn one refused (logged). `GetKillcam()` has the id, the victim, killer, killer's client, death and first ticks, chunks received and ready, bytes; `GetKillcamProgress()` is 0..1 counting the blobs on their way, 1 once complete. Logged `Net: killcam N received: ...` when complete. The receiver doesn't play anything: the `KillcamPlayer` does (below).

**Playing (phase 3, `Engine/KillcamPlayer.h`):** on the victim's side a `KillcamPlayer` watches the live world's receiver. Once a new killcam's first chunk is in (`Settings::AutoPlay`), it makes a second `World` (flagged `IsReplayWorld`) with its own `NetCore`, lets its owner's `Populate` fill it (the live scene's placed entities, so the placed NetIds line up; presentation cores; the script glue), starts it and plays the stream into it through ME-316's playback path: `NetCore::StartPlayback` over a `NetKillcamSource` (the receiver's `NetReplayMemorySource`, shared, so chunks still streaming in reach the playback: the "replay transport"). Nothing is predicted and every entity follows the stream, interpolated (`NetReplayPlayback::SetStreaming`: each `Update` picks up the chunks added since, and playing onto the last tick there is waits on it, still playing, instead of pausing). Every frame `Update( live, frame seconds )` moves the playhead, poses the replay world (inside a `WorldScope` of it, so what spawns creates its scripts there) and puts the replay world's main camera at the killer's eye as its screen had the tick shown (ME-343). A client shows the others at its view tick (interpolated, about 12–16 ticks behind the tick it predicts its own pawn at and aims in, over loopback in HeroShooter; its commands carry it and lag compensation rewinds to it), so the world at tick T was on the killer's screen while its client was at the tick whose command's view tick reached T: the camera takes that tick's recorded position (the killer's state) and aim, between it and the tick before (`KillcamPlayer::FindKillerView` over `KillerSample`s that a reader of its own reads ahead of the playhead from the same stream, as far as the death and what has arrived; `Status::KillerTick`), at the eye height above it: `Settings::EyeHeight`, else the game's (C#'s `Net.Killcam.EyeHeight`, the live world's `SingletonKillcam::EyeHeight`; HeroShooter's `HeroDefs.EyeHeight`, 0.7 m), else its `CharacterController`'s top less 0.15 m (1.6 m without one). While chunks are still streaming in, the playback waits where the killer's last tick in was looking (`NetReplayPlayback::SetHoldAt`, the view tick of the stream's last tick: the killer's view of anything later hasn't arrived), not on that last tick, so the camera is never the newest tick's eye over a later world; `Status::KillerViewTick` is the tick the view looked at (the tick shown, until the view holds at the kill). The killer's tick reaches the death tick, its last command (the kill), at the moment the killer saw it, with the victim where its screen had it; from there to the death tick the camera holds that view while the world plays on to the death. Showing the world and the killer's eye at the same tick put the victim 1–42° off the aim at the kill (9° on average over 19 HeroShooter kills; at 2 m one could be behind the camera) where the view tick puts it 1–4° off. A bot's view is where it is and faces at the tick shown. The killer's own meshes, models (a compact `Model` draws its parts itself) and text are hidden. The live world is never touched: it keeps its session, prediction and simulation, and the replay world's entities are its own (its own killer, victim and movers). It ends once the death tick has shown for `HoldSeconds` (1 s) with the stream whole (`played`), on `Skip` (`skipped`), when a newer killcam starts (`replaced`), when the live session ends or `Stop` (`stopped`), or when the playback errs or waits on a chunk for `MaxStallSeconds` (3 s; `failed: ...`); the replay world goes at once. `KillcamEvent` (`Started` / `Ended` with the reason, its `Source` the live world) is fired on the main thread; logged `Net: killcam N playing: killer #k, victim #v, ticks a-b (X s, c of n chunks in)` and `Net: killcam N ended (reason) after X s at tick T`. `GetStatus()` has the id, killer and victim NetIds, the killer's client, first and death ticks, the tick shown, elapsed and duration (first tick to death at the tick rate, plus the hold), whether the stream is whole, and the killer's view this frame (`HasView` only once the replay world's main camera entity has been put there: a world without one shows something else; its eye, aim, `KillerTick` and origin, and the victim's origin and where it lands in the frame, `VictimView` from `KillcamPlayer::Project`: x and y in -1..1 by the camera's field of view and aspect, the depth, `Inside`; `DescribeView()` as words). When the killer's tick reaches the death it logs `Net: killcam N death view at tick T: the camera at killer #k's eye at its tick K (x, y, z) aiming yaw Y pitch P, the killer at (...), the victim #v at (...) on screen at (x, y) D m ahead` (or `off screen`, `behind the camera`, `not in the world`), then the killer's own shot at the death tick nearest that aim (not a projectile's; `Status::HasShot`, `ShotOrigin`, `ShotDirection`): `; its shot from (x, y, z): the eye H m above it, D m beside it, the aim A degrees off it` (or `; no shot of its own at the death tick`), the killcam's eye against where the game fired from; `GetStats()` counts started, played, skipped, replaced, stopped, failed, frames stalled and death views. Each world's `SingletonKillcam` (the live one's and the replay world's) points at the player, `InReplayWorld` telling them apart. Killcam ids are a server's own (each server counts from 1; the receiver starts over at 0 on `Connect`), so the player forgets the newest id it has seen whenever the live session isn't live or its receiver has started over (`GetLastSeenId`): a killcam from the next server is never mistaken for one already played.

**Presenting it (the Player, `Engine::Run`):** the engine's `KillcamPlayer` plays what the game world's session receives. Its `Populate` gives the replay world a `SceneCore`, `CameraCore`, `RenderCore` and a presentation-only `ScriptCore` of its own (with the script glue), and loads the game world's scene into it again without its core list (`Scene::Load( world, false )`). The scene file isn't read at the death: once a client session is live, `UpdateKillcam` reads and parses it on the background lane (`Killcam Scene Read`, once per scene; a scene load forgets it), and each replay world loads a copy of that (logged `Net: killcam world: <scene> (read ahead), N entities in X ms`; `(read now)` if a death beat the read). Building the world is still on the main thread at the death: about 40 ms for HeroShooter's arena in Debug, 264 entities (stats `Killcam` and `Killcam Populate`). The replay world's NetCore takes the live session's command schema, so the recording's version check passes. Each frame, after the game world's late update (`Engine::UpdateKillcam`): the player's `Update` with the frame's game time, the replay world's `[ReplayWorld]` scripts (`Update`, `LateUpdate`), then exactly one world is presented: while a killcam plays the game world's `CameraCore` and `RenderCore` are off screen (`SetPresented( false )`: its cameras neither main nor rendering, its mesh commands emptied once and its render prep skipped, so the renderer's lights, decals, sprites and wind are the replay world's), the replay world's cameras update after the game world's (`LateUpdateKillcam`) and its `RenderCore` prepares the frame (it shares the renderer's mesh cache: `SetSharesRenderer`, it never clears it, only pops its own), the game world's particles keep simulating but draw nothing, and the audio listener is the replay world's (`AudioCore::SetListenerWorld`: its first `AudioListener`, else its main camera). The game's UI stays: RmlUi composites onto the game world's main camera, and its texture is handed to the replay world's each frame. The frame the killcam ends, the game world is presented again (its commands refilled by its next render prep). Not NET-12: one world is drawn a frame, never both. Havana doesn't play killcams yet (`AutoPlay` off: its Game View picks its own camera); a scene load or shutdown stops one and presents the game world again at once (the audio listener forgets the replay world's entity). `--killcam-capture file.png [--killcam-capture-at S|death]` (default 2 s) screenshots the screen that far into the first killcam, every killcam when the path has `{id}` (replaced by the killcam's id), `death` at the kill as the killer saw it (its tick at the death tick) and a negative S that long before it; it logs `Net: killcam N capture at tick T (S s in): the replay world presented, <the view as in the death view line> -> file` (the view only when the camera was placed there: `HasView`).

**From C#** (`Net.cs`): `Net.SendKillcam( victim, killer, seconds = 8f )` on the server (false off it, without the ring, for a bot victim or an unnetworked killer), and on a client `Net.Killcam`: `Id` (the newest one's, 0 for none: a new id is a new killcam), `Received` (whole), `Progress` (0..1), `Killer` and `Victim` (the entities in the script's world, `Entity.Null` once gone: in the replay world, its own copies). Playing (phase 3, `Net_GetKillcamPlayback` through the script world's `SingletonKillcam`): `IsPlaying`, `InReplayWorld` (the script runs in the killcam's replay world), `KillerId` and `VictimId` (NetIds), `Tick` (shown, fractional), `FirstTick`, `DeathTick`, `Elapsed`, `Duration`, `PlayingId` (the one playing, or the last one played; `Id` is the newest received, which can be a newer one for a frame), `Skip()` (`Net_SkipKillcam`: `KillcamPlayer::Skip` takes effect at the start of the player's next update, never while the replay world's own scripts run, so the same frame for a live world's script) and the events `Started` / `Ended` (EventBus `KillcamStarted( Id, KillerId, VictimId )` / `KillcamEnded( ..., Reason )`, which the live world's `ScriptCore` queues from `KillcamEvent`; the `event` accessors subscribe on the EventBus, so a script's handlers go with it and every handler goes on a hot reload). Scripts in the replay world: only `[ReplayWorld]` classes, presentation only (`Scripting-DotNet.md`, the script world). `Net.Killcam.EyeHeight` (`Net_SetKillcamEyeHeight` / `Net_GetKillcamEyeHeight`, ME-343) is where the game puts its eye above a pawn's origin, on the script world's `SingletonKillcam` (set it on the live world, e.g. a pawn's `OnStart`; below 0, the default, the engine guesses): the killer's eye in its killcams and the replay viewer's POV. `Replay.IsRecording` is true on a server keeping the ring, so `Replay.Event`s (kills, damage) go into it and into the killcams cut from it.

**In the reports:** the server's `--net-report` has `Killcams` (requested, encoded, completed, failed, cancelled, bytes, ticks, encode ms, pending), a client's `Killcam` (the newest: id, complete, chunks, bytes, victim, killer, ticks).

**Size and time** (the doctest below: 24 movers, 30 m relevancy, a 2-player 8 s killcam): 48 KB in 8 chunks, encoded in about 15 ms (Debug); at 150 ms RTT with 5% loss the first chunk is readable 0.1 to 0.35 s after the encode (a lost slice waits for its resend) and the whole of it 1.1 to 1.4 s after, the 8 s it covers well ahead of a death cam playing it. HeroShooter's 1.75 s killcams over loopback: 13–21 KB in 2 chunks, encoded in 6–10 ms, playing about 0.1 s after the death, the replay world built in 11–12 ms (Debug, 264 entities).

**In a game (HeroShooter, phase 4):** the game decides what a kill is and how long a killcam is. `Deathmatch.OnKill` on the server calls `Net.SendKillcam( victim, killer, 1.75f )` for a victim a player owns, sized to the respawn delay: 1.75 s plus the engine's 1 s hold on the death is 2.75 s, which ends (`played`) just inside the 3 s respawn after the 0.1 s it takes to arrive; a respawn cuts one still on screen (`Net.Killcam.Skip()`). Offline nothing is sent, so practice never shows one (a GameTest checks it). The HUD (`HudController`, `Hud.rml`) shows a KILLCAM overlay while `Net.Killcam.IsPlaying`: letterbox bars, the KILLCAM tag, `KILLED BY` the killer's name and hero in its colour (the killer's NetId matched to its pawn in the live world, the first frame the overlay is up for a `PlayingId`; a pawn gone since takes the kill feed's killer and hero), the killcam's progress (`Elapsed / Duration`), the respawn count and the skip key, in place of the "KILLED BY" banner. The `SkipKillcam` input action (Space, pad South; Interface context, so it works while gameplay input is dead) calls `Net.Killcam.Skip()`. Its pawns are `[ReplayWorld]` and present the recording (hidden once dead, the gun at the recorded pitch). It logs `HS: killcam start / overlay / skip / end` (the overlay line from the first frame the overlay is up, with the names it shows) and `HS: killcam cut at respawn`, which `Tools/MatchSmoke.py` checks per victim (below, **Proofs**).

**Limits:**

- **One killcam at a time, none resumed.** The receiver keeps the newest; a newer one replaces the one playing; a reconnect loses one in flight. `Skip` doesn't cancel a killcam still streaming (`CancelIncomingBlob`), so its last slices still arrive.
- **The replay world is rebuilt every death** (11–14 ms on HeroShooter's arena in Debug, the scene read ahead on the background lane) rather than kept between killcams; it has no `AudioCore` (the live world's sounds play, heard from the killer's eye), `AnimationCore` or particles, and shots and hit markers from the stream aren't drawn yet. Additive scenes aren't loaded into it, so a scene with them refuses killcams.
- **What the killer's client had, as the server judged it:** NET-13's relevancy from the killer's pawn after the fact, not what its snapshots actually carried (see the caveat below); only the killer's `[Predicted]` state rides the stream, so a script sees a victim's death through `[Replicated]` fields.
- **The eye is the recorded position plus the game's eye height:** the server's position at the killer's tick, not its client's predicted one (they differ by its corrections; MatchSmoke finds the eye level with HeroShooter's shot origin and 0.06 m beside it, the medians over 19 kills: the shot is fired mid-tick, before the tick's move), and the killer's own view of itself (its camera's smoothing of corrections, a third-person guard camera) isn't recorded. A game that doesn't set `Net.Killcam.EyeHeight` gets the capsule's top less 0.15 m.
- **The first ticks of a player's killcam** are shown from the killer's first tick in the stream (before its first view tick there is no tick of the killer's that looked at them): the first 12-16 ticks over loopback.
- **C# shares statics across worlds:** `Scripts.All`, `Net.Events` and a script's statics are process-wide; `[ReplayWorld]` scripts check `Net.Killcam.InReplayWorld`.
- **Havana** receives killcams but doesn't play them (`AutoPlay` off).
- **Bandwidth:** `NetBlobSettings::LinkBytesPerSecond` (128 KB/s) isn't set from the session or the command line yet; an 8 s killcam of a small match is about 50 KB.

### UDP and dedicated servers (NET-5)

**`UdpTransport`:**

- **Socket:** one non-blocking IPv4 UDP socket, bound to a port (0 picks a free one) on every interface or one address.
- **Receive:** returns false once nothing more has arrived. Datagrams over 1500 bytes are skipped (`NetConnection` packets are at most 1200).
- **Addresses:** a `NetAddress` is `(ip << 16) | port`, so the address a client resolves from `--connect` is bit for bit the `From` its replies carry. `Resolve` asks for IPv4 only, so `localhost` can't come back as `::1`.
- **Platforms:**
  - Winsock on Windows: `PCH.h` includes `winsock2.h` before `Windows.h`, `Ws2_32.lib` links by pragma, and `SIO_UDP_CONNRESET` is off.
  - BSD sockets on Linux and macOS.
- **Binding:** no `SO_REUSEADDR`, so a second process can't silently share a port.
- **Conditions:** `--net-lag` and the other `--net-*` options wrap the socket in a `ConditionedTransport`. `NetCore` drives `Update` from `Receive`, `EndTick` and `Flush`, so held packets go out at frame resolution.

**The Server build** (`CommonTarget.Mode.Server` → `server_<platform>_<optimization>`, e.g. `make -f MitchGame.make Game_EntryPoint config=server_linux_debug` → `.build/Server_linux_Debug/`):

- **Defines:** `DEFINE_ME_HEADLESS`. Debug and Release keep `ME_TOOLS`, so scripts still build beside the exe, while `ME_IMGUI` and `ME_GAME_TOOLS` are off when headless.
- **Scripts of a shared executable:** the headless Player running a C#-only project builds its scripts into `<project>/.tmp/Scripts/Player-Server-<Config>/` (and `--artifacts-path .tmp/dotnet-Server`), apart from the client Players' `Player-<Config>/`, so a server and its clients on one machine never `dotnet build` into the same folder.
- **Hosts by default:** a headless build with neither `--server` nor `--connect` hosts on the default port (`NetLaunch::IsHosting`), so a shipped server starts with no arguments (ME-308: Build Game's `Server` profile ships it as `<Product>-Server`, `Building.md`). Ctrl+C or SIGTERM stops it through the engine's quit path (`Engine/QuitSignals.h`: the .NET runtime's own SIGTERM handling exited the process mid-frame and crashed the shutdown), so its clients get a Disconnect.
- **Window:** `Engine::Init` creates a `NullWindow` instead of an SDL window.
- **Renderer:** runs bgfx's **Noop** backend (`RendererCreationSettings::Type`). Noop loads the platform's own shader binaries (`GetPlatformString`: spirv / metal / dx11) and draws nothing.
  - Frames only advance bgfx: no mesh/sprite command prep, particles, culling or passes (ME-286). LostTemple averages 6.0 ms a frame instead of 9.9, Playground 0.26 instead of 0.76.
  - Cameras and terrain still update, since terrain streams its colliders.
- **Pace:** with no display to wait on, a headless run caps itself at one frame per tick (`1 / FixedTimeStep`) unless `--max-fps` or the config's `MaxFrameRate` says otherwise.
- **UI:** RmlUi gets no renderer, so it never builds a context: no documents load. Values set through `UI.Model` are kept, unread.
- **Audio:** none. FMOD compiles out (`ME_FMOD` needs `!ME_HEADLESS`) and isn't linked or shipped (`CommonTarget.HasAudio`, ME-287): a dedicated server needs no `libfmod`.
- **Where it builds:** Win64, macOS and Linux, never UWP.

**Playing locally** (`Tools/RunNetLocal.py`, ME-289):

- **What it does:** starts the Server build, waits for it to host, then opens N client windows side by side.

**Script hot reload pauses during a session** (ME-311, tools builds):

- **Why:** every tools process watches `Assets/` and rebuilds its C# when a `.cs` changes. With a server and clients on one machine, one edit started several `dotnet build`s at once (the clients share an output folder), and the reload changed the script schema the handshake checks: later joiners were turned away (`scripts`) while the peers already in played on with the old scripts.
- **What happens:** while `NetCore::IsSessionLive()` (hosting until `Close`, or a client connecting or connected), `Engine::PollAssetChanges` doesn't call `ScriptEngine::RequestReload` for a `.cs` change, and a build already running when the session began isn't swapped in (`PollReload( false )` keeps a successful build waiting and logs `Scripts: built; the reload waits for the network session to end`; a failed one is reported as usual). `ScriptReloadGate` (`Source/Engine/ScriptReloadGate.h`) decides, and logs once each: `Scripts: hot reload paused for the network session (edits rebuild once it ends)`, the first held edit, and `Scripts: the network session ended; hot reload resumes` (with `, rebuilding the scripts edited during it` when one was held).
- **When it ends:** a client whose session is over (it left, was rejected, timed out or was disconnected) or a server after `Close` resumes, and rebuilds once if anything changed meanwhile. To pick up an edit, restart the session (RunNetLocal again).
- **Builds take turns:** `ScriptEngine::BuildScripts` holds `.tmp/ScriptBuild.lock` (an advisory `flock` / `LockFileEx`, released on exit or crash, not inherited by dotnet) for the whole `dotnet build`, so a project's processes build one at a time: they share `.tmp/MitchEngine.props` and the dotnet artifacts folder, and sibling clients one output folder. Clients whose session ends together (the server went away) both resume and rebuild; the second logs `Scripts: waiting for another process's script build`, then `another process built them meanwhile; not building again`, and reloads the first's DLL. The same goes for clients started together on stale scripts.
- **A peer started after a held edit** isn't paused yet: tools builds rebuild stale scripts at startup (`ScriptEngine::NeedsBuild`), so a client launched mid-session after a `.cs` edit loads the new scripts and the paused server turns it away (`scripts`) until the session restarts. It can't know which scripts the server has (a fresh server built them too), so this is by design; restart the session after editing scripts.
- **Unchanged:** without a session (the editor, offline play) every edit reloads as before, and the inspector's **Rebuild && Reload Scripts** button always works: it's an explicit request (`RequestReload( true )`), so its build swaps in through the hold.

**The smoke test** (`Tools/RunNetSmoke.py`, the RunChecks "Networking smoke" step):

1. Starts the Server build on a free port (`--server 0`, read back from its log) on `Assets/Scenes/Tests/NetTest.lvl` with `--net-exit-when-empty`. NetTest has six networked boxes that fall and settle in view, and a `NetSpin` cube behind the camera that turns every fixed step, so every tick has a snapshot to send.
2. Waits for the server to host.
3. Runs a client through `Headless.sh` for 900 frames at `--max-fps 60`, then captures and leaves.
4. **Passes when:**
   - the client connected and stayed connected for at least 80% of its run, with no timeouts on either side and no bgfx asserts in either log;
   - snapshots arrived within 5% of the tick rate;
   - the server counted a clean Disconnect and exited on its own;
   - the capture matches `Assets/Scenes/Tests/Reference/NetTest.png`. The same reference serves the standalone test-scene capture: the settled boxes land in the same places offline.
5. `--net-args` passes options to both sides (e.g. `--net-args "--net-lag 40 --net-loss 2%"`), and `--update` takes a new reference.

- **Quantization:** floats at 4, 10, 16 and 24 bits come back within half a step, vectors at 20 bits, and 500 random rotations at 12 bits (|dot| > 0.99999).
- **Reliable under a bad link:** 600 messages one way and 150 the other, over 40 ms ± 15 ms with 20% loss, 10% duplicates and 10% reordering, arrive complete, in order and once. Unreliable messages arrive at most once (most of them). The loss estimate reads 10–32%.
- **Blobs (ME-323):**
  - **Clean link:** blobs of 1 byte, one slice, one slice and a byte, 3000 bytes, 200 KB and the 4 MB cap arrive whole and byte for byte, four at a time with the rest queued, the small ones not held behind the big one; partway, the receiver's progress shows the bytes so far, all from the start. ## How to Extend

- **A real transport (Steam, NET-9):** implement `INetTransport` as `UdpTransport` does. `Update` can stay empty (`NetCore` calls it anyway), and `NetConnection` doesn't change. Map the transport's peer identities to stable `NetAddress`es: replies must come from the address the client connected to.
- **Bigger messages:** need fragmentation (a reliable slice stream). Until then, keep replicated state under 1 KB a message. Snapshots defer entities past their 900-byte budget; NET-4 adds priorities.
- **A replicated component field:** add `.Replicated()` to its `ME_FIELD`. Clients apply it from the snapshot at the render tick.
- **A networked script:** mark fields `[Replicated]` / `[Predicted]` and methods `[ServerRpc]` / `[ClientRpc]` in a `partial` `Script`, write `On{Name}` handlers, read input with `Net.Command(Entity)`, and mark the actions it reads `"Networked": true` in the `.inputactions`.
- **A new net field kind:** add it to `NetGenerator`'s kind mapping and to `NetWriter` / `NetReader` (fixed-size for state), then update the generator tests (the kind name is part of the schema hash).

## Caveats & Fragility

- **Clock:** the connection trusts the clock it's given. Pass the same monotonic `now` to `SendPacket` and `ReceivePacket`.
- **Reliable window:** a stalled link holds at most 1024 messages in flight. Messages queued beyond that wait in memory, unbounded, until acks free the window.
- **No encryption or authentication:** the protocol id only filters strays.
- **NetSchema is positional:** both sides must have the same components with the same replicated fields, in the same order. The C++ side isn't hashed into the handshake (the build hash covers it for the same game); C# net fields and RPCs are (NET-6).
- **Script prediction is exact:** `[Predicted]` bytes compare bit for bit. Float code that differs across platforms (a Windows client on a Linux server) would read as corrections; quantize such state, or keep it in ints.
- **Hot reload during a session** is paused (ME-311, above). Forced from the inspector's button, it changes the schema hash for new joiners only, and drops the generated state of reloaded instances back to their saved public fields. A peer launched after a held edit builds the new scripts at startup and is turned away until the session restarts.
- **Spawned prefabs:** clients create spawned entities from `NetIdentity::Prefab`, which must exist on the client.
- **A second world drives its own `NetCore` (ME-297).** Per-world state is the world's (its `NetCore`, `SingletonInput`, main camera), but `Engine::Run` drives only the game world's: a killcam or replay world's owner calls its `NetCore`'s frame hooks (`Receive` / `BeginTick` / `EndTick` / `WriteInterpolated` / `Flush`, `BeginPresentation` / `EndPresentation` for NET-7's visual offset) and samples its input itself. `NetLaunch`'s command-line replay viewer, Net Debugger and `--replay-check` work on the game world's `NetCore`. On the C# side two pieces are still process-wide: `Net.Events` (ME-291's predicted-event ledger, keyed by entity index and generation, which two worlds' entities can share) and the action-index cache (correct across worlds, since layout versions are unique, but it rebuilds whenever scripts of two worlds alternate). Presentation still has one main camera per process: each world's main camera marks its `CameraData` `IsMain` in the renderer's one camera cache, the renderer presents the first `IsMain` entry and skips the others (and `RenderCore` faces world text to the first), so a second world that renders needs its owner to pick the presenting world, as the Player does for a killcam (`CameraCore` / `RenderCore::SetPresented`, ME-323), or draw the other world's camera to a texture (NET-12).
- **UDP is IPv4 only** (a `NetAddress` is 64 bits).
  - On a multi-homed server bound to every interface, a reply can leave from another address than the one the client dialled, and the client then ignores it. Bind one address, or connect to the address it answers from.
  - There is no NAT traversal; Steam SDR (NET-9) brings that.
- **Ticks follow the engine's fixed steps.** Don't give a server `--frame-time`: its ticks then follow frames rather than the clock, so any stall or speed-up shows up as client resyncs. Real-time servers step by the clock and catch up (`KeepsBacklog`), measuring slack against the caught-up tick. A hitch longer than `NetServer::kMaxCatchUpSeconds` (0.5 s) loses ticks for good, which clients see as one resync back. A server too slow to keep up keeps no backlog, and its clients resync back every second or so (each time they get 8 ticks ahead); that's the price of their input not waiting on a backlog.
- **The harness mirrors `Engine::Run`'s catch-up** (the 0.5 s bound, dropping a backlog it can't run, and with `ServerStepsPerFrame` 8 steps a frame, a frame a tick), but reads packets before every step rather than once a frame. Change both together.
- **Mixed builds:** protocol 7 changed what reports mean, not just their bytes. The build hash folds the protocol in, so a protocol-6 client or server is rejected (`"version mismatch: protocol build"`) rather than misread. Protocol 8 (the blob channel) only adds a trailer older peers skip, but it's folded in the same way.
- **Blobs share the link, not the snapshot budget.** `NetBlobSettings::LinkBytesPerSecond` is per connection and counts every packet; nothing yet sets it from the session or the command line, and a link slower than it (the 128 KB/s default) is only protected by the loss it causes. An 8 s killcam of a small match is about 50 KB (the doctest), well within the default; a busy one (dozens of movers in view, every pawn's `[Replicated]` fields changing each tick) hasn't been measured yet.
- **The jitter floor follows the round trip's deviation,** which a step in latency inflates for a moment (so does the first sample: the deviation starts at half the round trip, and a two-way latency spike). The target it raises eases down at a quarter tick a second, so after a big latency change (or on a high-latency link's first seconds) the buffer is deeper than the link needs for a while.
- **Own hitches buy no buffer.** A client hitch's misses on the ticks it made late never grow the target (ME-313), so a client that hitches often (a pause every second or two) misses on every one of them rather than buffering deeper. Hitch detection is by send time: a frame two ticks past the usual counts, so at 20 fps a 0.1 s frame is a hitch and a 0.07 s one isn't.
- **Two-frame hitches aren't told apart.** At 60 fps a hitch under about 0.045 s looks like the frame-to-frame wobble dilation causes (a frame that runs no tick, then one that runs two): its miss grows the target, and its round-trip sample passes too, so on a 20 ms link a 0.03–0.04 s hitch takes the target from 1 to 3 for a few seconds. One tick past the usual instead of two read dilation's empty frames as hitches.
- **A hitch the engine drops time on costs a resync.** Past 8 steps and a frame (0.155 s at 60 Hz, by the frame's phase) the clock comes back behind and jumps forward (**Behind**, **Back from its own hitch**): filled, and the target unchanged, but the ticks dropped are skipped rather than simulated, and the server's smoothed slack takes up to a quarter second to follow a jump under 4 ticks. On a link whose target is a few ticks deep (jitter, loss), the slack absorbs the drop instead.
- **Spikes and the shrink:** spikes further apart than `ShrinkAfterSeconds` (3 s) let the target ease a little between them, so a spike every few seconds still costs a miss now and then (0–1 a 100 ms spike once the buffer has grown). Spikes longer than `MaxSlack` (8 ticks: 133 ms at 60 Hz) cost what the deepest buffer can't cover, every time; longer than that plus the usual wait, they're outages and don't grow it at all.
- **Killcams cut the ring as the server had it.** Relevancy is NET-13's rule from the killer's pawn applied after the fact, not what the killer's client was actually sent (the byte budget and priorities aren't replayed): an entity in view that its snapshots starved is still in the killcam. An entity out of view since before the ring's oldest tick holds its pose at that tick, not the older one the killer's client may have had. A killcam needs the victim's owner to be connected through the whole send; nothing resumes one after a reconnect. The receiver keeps the newest killcam only.
